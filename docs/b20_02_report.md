## 0. Plain terms, and the verdict up front

Two exclusion theorems closed the *rank-threshold* family: the ideals `J_k` generated by the
`(r_det(k)+1)`-minors of the Macaulay matrix `M_k(F)`, which is the first differential `d_1` of
the Koszul complex of the five (or sixteen) partials of `F`, lie in the common ideal
`I(Y_det) ∩ I(Y_pad)` in every internal grading `k`. Nothing was known about the *higher*
differentials `d_2, d_3, ...` of the same complex in five variables (Candidate A), and nothing
was known about projective duality beyond the one dual-dimension construction (Candidate B).

This slot chooses **Candidate A** and delivers a **rigorous closure** of it in five variables:

> **Theorem 6.4.** In five variables, for every `j >= 2` and every internal grading `k`, the
> maximal rank of the `j`-th Koszul differential `d_j^{(k)}` on the determinant closure `D45`
> equals its maximal rank on the whole space of quartics. Consequently the rank-threshold
> ideal of `d_j^{(k)}` on `D45` is the **zero ideal**: the higher differentials of the Koszul
> complex supply **no equation of `D45` at all** in five variables, in any grading, and the
> reversal `rank d_2(pad) > rank d_2(det)` contemplated in corrigendum item C5 cannot occur
> there.

The reason is short. A generic five-variable determinantal quartic has a finite projective
singular locus (its corank-two points), so its Jacobian ideal has grade 4 in a ring with five
partials, and the Koszul complex of five forms whose ideal has grade 4 has homology only in
positions 0 and 1 (depth sensitivity). Every rank beyond `d_1` is then an alternating sum of
dimensions that does not depend on `F`. Padding has grade 2, so its complex has extra homology
and its higher ranks are *lower*: the determinant is maximally nondegenerate for every `d_j`,
`j >= 2`, and there is nothing for a minor to detect.

Status of the theorem: **PROVED unconditionally at every internal grading where an explicit
integer pencil attains the generic rank (certified in §7 for the gradings listed there);
PROVED CONDITIONAL for all `k` at once** on the depth-sensitivity theorem for Koszul homology
(Bruns–Herzog Thm 1.6.17, UNREAD in primary text in this session; stated at statement level
in Dimca arXiv:1210.1795v4 Remark 3.5, PRIMARY, hashed) and on `grade = height` in a
polynomial ring (Bruns–Herzog Cor. 2.1.4, UNREAD). No numerical certificate depends on either.

**What this does not establish.** No gap, no cell, no equation nonzero on padding, no
statement about sixteen variables beyond §8, and no statement about Candidate B beyond the
non-applicability assessment of §4 (Candidate B is not chosen and is left ABSENT, with the

## 3. Candidate A — higher differentials: why the three exclusions do not apply

**(A-i) Theorems A and B do not apply.** They are statements about `rank d_1^{(k)}` and about
the ideal generated by the `(r_det(k)+1)`-minors of `d_1^{(k)}`. A minor of `d_j^{(k)}`, `j >= 2`,
is a polynomial in the entries of a *different* matrix (`K_j(k) -> K_{j-1}(k)`, entries linear
in `F`), and the ideal `J_k^{(j)}(Y)` is a different `GL_N`-stable ideal. The only relation the
first-differential theorems impose on the higher ranks is the identity of C5,

    rank d_1^{(k)} = dim K_1(k) - rank d_2^{(k)} - dim H_1(F)_k,

which constrains the **sum** `rank d_2 + dim H_1` and not its summands. So Theorems A and B
neither contain `J_k^{(j)}(Y)` for `j >= 2` in the common ideal nor determine `r_Y^{(j)}(k)`.
(The corrigendum, C5–C7, says exactly this and lists `d_2` at every `k` for `N = 5` as never
computed.)

**(A-ii) Astra's theorems do not apply.** They live on the *source* side: linear maps on the
finite-dimensional space `M` of semi-invariant polynomial functions of degree `4d` on the
pencil space `W^k`, and the arc condition `C` on such functions. A minor of `d_j^{(k)}` is a
polynomial `g` in the quartic coefficients `c_alpha` (coefficient side). Its pull-back
`phi^* g` is, tautologically, in the descended space `E = H ∘ phi`, on which every `N_w`
vanishes by Astra Prop. 7.2; Astra's theorems say nothing about the value of `g` on `P5` or
`Y_pad`, which is the only question here. The two statements are in different categories
(coefficient-side ideals versus source-side tests), as C12 records ("complementary").

**(A-iii) The reason Candidate A could escape the reversal, as stated by C5.** Padding's
larger singular locus makes `dim H_1(pad)_k` larger; the C5 identity allows
`rank d_2(pad) > rank d_2(det)` provided `dim H_1(pad) < dim H_1(det)` by more than the
`d_1`-rank difference. Whether this happens is a fact about Koszul homology in positions
`>= 2`, which the Hilbert function of the Milnor algebra does not determine. **This is the
escape to be tested, and §6 tests it by theory: in five variables it is impossible, because
the determinant's complex has no homology in positions `>= 2` at all.** So Candidate A does
*not* escape the reversal in five variables — but the outcome is stronger than a wrong-way
statistic: it is a closure with zero ideal, not a "padding satisfies it too".

## 6. Closure of Candidate A in five variables

Throughout §6, `N = 5`, `S = C[x_1..x_5]`, `F` a quartic, `K(F)` its Koszul complex with the
grading of §1.2. All dimension formulas: `dim K_j(k) = C(5, j) . dim S_{k-3j}`.

### 6.1 Generic ranks, and the rank-threshold ideal of a differential

**Lemma 6.1 (generic ranks).** For every `j >= 1` and `k >= 0`,

    rho_j(k) = sum_{i=j}^{5} (-1)^{i-j} dim K_i(k),

and `rho_j(k)` is attained at every quartic `F` with `H_i(F)_k = 0` for all `i >= 1`
(in particular at every smooth quartic).

*Proof.* If all `H_i(F)_k` vanish for `i >= 1` then `ker d_i^{(k)} = im d_{i+1}^{(k)}` for
`i >= 1` and `rank d_i = dim K_i(k) - rank d_{i+1}`, with `rank d_5 = dim K_5(k)` because
`d_5(e_{12345} (x) g) = sum ± f_i g e_{I \ i}` is injective (`S` is a domain, `f_i != 0`);
the displayed alternating sum follows by downward induction. The partials of a smooth
quartic form a regular sequence, so the complex is exact in positions `>= 1` (standard;
this is the only property of smooth quartics used). Smooth quartics form a dense open subset
of `Sym^4`; the maximal-rank locus of the matrix `d_j^{(k)}(F)`, whose entries are linear in
`F`, is also dense open; the two meet, so the maximum over `Sym^4` equals the value at a
smooth quartic, which is the displayed sum. ∎

**Lemma 6.2 (rank-threshold ideal of `d_j`; the `d_j`-version of the parent's Lemma 3.1).**
For a closed cone `Y ⊆ Sym^4` and any `j, k`: `J_k^{(j)}(Y) ⊆ I(Y)`. If `r_Y^{(j)}(k) = rho_j(k)`
then `J_k^{(j)}(Y) = (0)`.

*Proof.* Every `(r+1)`-minor of a matrix of rank `<= r` vanishes, and `r^{(j)}(F, k) <= r_Y^{(j)}(k)`
for all `F in Y` by definition of the maximum. If the maximum equals `rho_j(k)`, every
`(rho_j(k)+1)`-minor vanishes at every quartic, i.e. is the zero polynomial. ∎

So the entire content of "Candidate A, rank-threshold sense, five variables" is the integer
comparison `r_{D45}^{(j)}(k)` versus `rho_j(k)`, and, if they differ, the comparison with
`r_{P5}^{(j)}(k)`.

### 6.2 A generic pencil has a finite singular locus

**Lemma 6.3.** Let `Lambda ⊂ Mat_4(C)` be a generic five-dimensional subspace, `(A_1..A_5)` a
basis, and `F = det(sum_i x_i A_i)`. Then the projective singular locus of `X_F ⊂ P^4` is
`{ x : rank(sum_i x_i A_i) <= 2 } = P(Lambda) ∩ P(Z_2)`, a nonempty finite set. Consequently
`V(J_F) ⊂ C^5` is a finite union of lines through the origin, `ht(J_F) = 4`, and (in the
polynomial ring, where height equals grade) `grade(J_F) = 4`.

*Proof.* (a) The gradient of `det` at `A` is the cofactor matrix `cof(A)` (`d det / d a_{pq} =
(-1)^{p+q} times the complementary minor`); it vanishes iff `rank A <= 2`, and has rank one
iff `rank A = 3`. (b) `d_i F(x) = <cof(A(x)), A_i>` with `A(x) = sum x_i A_i` and `<., .>` the
trace pairing; so `x in Sing X_F` iff `cof(A(x)) ⊥ Lambda`, i.e. iff either `rank A(x) <= 2`,
or `rank A(x) = 3` and `Lambda ⊆ cof(A(x))^⊥ = T_{A(x)} X_det` (a hyperplane). (c) *The second
case does not occur for generic `Lambda`.* The incidence `Psi = { (A, Lambda) : A in X_det`
smooth, `A in P(Lambda)`, `Lambda ⊆ T_A X_det }` fibres over the 14-dimensional smooth locus of
`X_det` with fibre `Gr(4, 14)` (five-dimensional subspaces of the 15-dimensional `T_A` that
contain the line `A`), of dimension `40`; so `dim Psi = 54 < 55 = dim Gr(5, 16)`, and the
projection of `Psi` to `Gr(5, 16)` is not dominant: a generic `Lambda` is tangent to `X_det` at
no smooth point. (d) *The first case is finite and nonempty for generic `Lambda`.* `P(Z_2)` is
irreducible of dimension `11` in `P^15` and `P(Lambda)` has dimension `4`, and `11 + 4 >= 15`,
so they meet (projective dimension theorem); the incidence `Phi = { (A, Lambda) : A in P(Z_2),
A in P(Lambda) }` is irreducible of dimension `11 + dim Gr(4, 15) = 11 + 44 = 55 = dim Gr(5, 16)`
and maps onto `Gr(5, 16)`, so its generic fibre `P(Lambda) ∩ P(Z_2)` has dimension `55 - 55 = 0`
(fibre-dimension theorem). (e) Hence `Sing X_F` is a nonempty finite set, `V(J_F)` is the
affine cone over it, of dimension `1`, so `ht(J_F) = 5 - 1 = 4`, and `grade = ht` in the
Cohen–Macaulay ring `S`. ∎

The two textbook facts used in (d) (projective dimension theorem; fibre-dimension theorem)
and in (e) (`grade = height` in a Cohen–Macaulay ring, Bruns–Herzog Cor. 2.1.4) are standard;
none is read in primary text in this session and they are labelled in §9. Lemma 6.3 does
**not** use Kleiman transversality and does not need the singular points to be nodes or to
number twenty; it needs only finiteness. (Their being twenty nodes for a generic pencil is
the parent's ADOPTED Kleiman input, consistent with `deg P(Z_2) = 20`; it is not used here.)
Since the set of pencils whose span is a generic `Lambda` in the sense of (c)–(d) is a dense
open subset of `(Mat_4)^5`, its image under `phi` is dense in `D45`.

### 6.3 Depth sensitivity of the Koszul complex

**(T1) Depth sensitivity.** *For forms `f_1..f_n` in a Noetherian ring `R` generating an ideal
`I` with `grade(I) = g`, the Koszul homology satisfies `H_j(f_1..f_n; R) = 0` for `j > n - g`,
and `H_{n-g} != 0`.* — Bruns–Herzog, *Cohen–Macaulay rings*, Thm 1.6.17 (also Eisenbud,
*Commutative algebra*, Thm 17.4; Matsumura, *Commutative ring theory*, Thm 16.8). **UNREAD** in
primary text in this session. Statement-level PRIMARY confirmation: Dimca arXiv:1210.1795v4
(hash in §1.1), Remark 3.5, p. 8 of the PDF: for a hypersurface `D : f = 0 ⊂ P^n` with only
isolated singularities, computing the double complex "along the lines, we get nonzero terms
only on the last two columns, which correspond to the Čech complex for `H^n(K^*(f))` (resp.
`H^{n+1}(K^*(f))`)" — i.e. the Koszul complex of the `n+1` partials has cohomology only in
the top two positions; and Remark 3.6, citing Bruns–Herzog Thm 1.6.16 p. 50 for "the first
nonzero cohomology group in the Koszul complex". In homological indexing with `n + 1 = 5`
forms this is exactly `H_j = 0` for `j >= 2`.

**(T2) `grade = height` in a polynomial ring.** Bruns–Herzog Cor. 2.1.4. **UNREAD**; standard.

Both are load-bearing for the all-`k` statement and make it CONDITIONAL (G14). Neither is
load-bearing for any grading at which an explicit pencil attains `rho_j(k)`, by Lemma 6.2
alone.

### 6.4 The theorem

**Theorem 6.4 (closure of Candidate A, five variables).** For every `j >= 2` and every `k >= 0`,

    r_{D45}^{(j)}(k) = rho_j(k),   hence   J_k^{(j)}(D45) = (0).

Equivalently: for a generic `F in D45`, `H_j(F)_k = 0` for all `j >= 2` and all `k`, and
every rank in the Koszul complex of `F` other than `rank d_1^{(k)}` is independent of `F`:

    rank d_j^{(k)}(F) = rho_j(k)  (j >= 2),      dim H_1(F)_k = dim K_1(k) - rho_2(k) - rank M_k(F),
    dim H_0(F)_k = dim S_k - rank M_k(F).

*Proof.* Take `F = phi(A)` with `span(A) = Lambda` generic as in Lemma 6.3, so `grade(J_F) = 4`
by Lemma 6.3 and (T2). By (T1) with `n = 5`, `g = 4`: `H_j(F) = 0` for `j > 1`, in every
grading. The argument of Lemma 6.1 (downward induction from `rank d_5 = dim K_5`, using
`ker d_i = im d_{i+1}` for `i >= 2`) gives `rank d_j^{(k)}(F) = rho_j(k)` for `j >= 2`. Since
`r^{(j)}(F, k) <= r_{D45}^{(j)}(k) <= rho_j(k)`, equality holds throughout; Lemma 6.2 gives
`J_k^{(j)}(D45) = (0)`. The two displayed identities are the C5 identity at `j = 1` and the
definition of `H_0`. ∎

**Corollary 6.5 (no reversal in five variables).** For every quartic `F'` (in particular every
point of `P5`, and every actual five-variable padding restriction), every `j >= 2` and every `k`:
`rank d_j^{(k)}(F') <= rho_j(k) = r_{D45}^{(j)}(k)`. The inequality `rank d_2(pad) > rank d_2(det)`
of C5 cannot hold between the maxima over `P5` and `D45` at any `k`. Moreover the padding
complex has strictly more homology: for `F' = ell C` with `ell` not dividing `C` and `C` a
smooth cubic, in coordinates with `ell = x_1` one has `J_{F'} = (C, x_1 . J_C)` up to the
Euler relation, so `V(J_{F'}) = V(x_1, C) ∪ V(J_C) = V(x_1, C) ∪ {0}` has dimension `3`,
`grade(J_{F'}) = 2`, and by (T1) `H_3(F') != 0` in some grading while `H_4 = H_5 = 0`. (This
last sentence is CONDITIONAL on (T1) like the theorem; the pilots record the padding-side
ranks at the computed gradings as data, not as a claim.)

**Corollary 6.6 (what the whole complex knows in five variables).** For generic `F in D45`, the
family of all ranks `{ rank d_j^{(k)}(F) }_{j, k}` is a function of the single family
`{ rank M_k(F) }_k` already governed by Theorem B. There is no independent statistic anywhere
in the Koszul complex of a generic five-variable determinantal quartic: not in `d_2`, not in
any `d_j`, not in the Betti-type data of C5 (`dim H_j`, `j >= 2`, all zero).

### 6.5 Exact scope

Covered by Theorem 6.4: the rank-threshold ideals `J_k^{(j)}(D45)` for every `j >= 2` and every
internal grading `k`, in five variables — they are zero. Covered by Corollary 6.5: the
impossibility of the C5 reversal in five variables.

Not covered, in the same sense as the corrigendum's C1 exclusion: individual minors of
`d_j^{(k)}` of size `<= rho_j(k)` that vanish on `D45` for a zero-pattern or support reason,
and their linear combinations (nothing is claimed about them either way); the `GL_5`-module
structure of `H_1(F)` (the syzygy module beyond the Koszul relations) — its *dimension* in
each grading is determined by `rank M_k(F)`, but its isotypic decomposition and the
equations it might supply through non-threshold mechanisms are not addressed; sixteen
variables (§8); Candidate B (§4).

Not a gap, not a cell, not an equation nonzero on padding. A closure with zero ideal is the
strongest negative the rank-threshold reading of Candidate A admits, and it is a negative.
