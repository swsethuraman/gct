## Why this slot exists

A pencil `L ∈ Hom(ℂ⁵, ℂ⁴⊗ℂ⁴)` is a 5-tuple of 4×4 matrices under the left-right `SL₄×SL₄`
action. The coefficients of `det(Σ xᵢ Lᵢ)` are degree-4 semi-invariants, and the coordinate ring
of the determinant closure is the subalgebra they generate.

- The record has an exact map for that subalgebra's multiplicities: session 56's Foulkes map,
  whose rank is `mult_det`. It is priced out from degree 5 on (B27-05 at
  `96a8074d:results/b27_05/REPORT.md`).
- The full semi-invariant count cannot drop below the ambient count for `k ≤ 10` (session 38).
- B27-01 left an unresolved 49-dimensional class of padding points (class iii): `lC` with `C`
  singular, not in `D₃,₅` and containing no plane. These are excluded as literal determinants,
  but their closure membership is open (`docs/b27_01_report.md`).

**This slot reads the literature. It does not do new mathematics or propose mechanisms.**

## Ladder

**2a — Image multiplicities.** What is known about computing, or bounding from above, the
multiplicity of an irreducible `GL₅`-module (or the dimension of a `GL₅` weight space) in the
subalgebra generated by the lowest-degree semi-invariants of `M_m(ℂ)ⁿ` under the left-right
action, **as distinct from the full semi-invariant ring**? Relevant areas include:
- matrix semi-invariants and their spanning sets (Derksen–Weyman, Domokos–Zubkov,
  Schofield–van den Bergh);
- degree bounds (Derksen–Makam);
- coordinate rings of determinant orbit closures;
- Foulkes and plethysm maps.

For each source found, give the exact statement, its hypotheses, and whether it applies at
`m = 4`, `n = 5`, degree `4k` for `k = 8–10`. Name any algorithm with a stated complexity.

**2b — First-order boundary points.** What is known about the boundary of `det_n` orbit closures,
and in particular about first-order limits `tr(adj(M₀)·M₁)` with `det M₀ ≡ 0`? Does any source
decide whether a quartic `l·C` with `C` a singular cubic is such a limit, or lies in the closure
of `D₄,₅` by another explicit construction? Say which results are for `n = 3` only.

**2c — Verdict per question.** For each of 2a and 2b, choose one:
- **(A)** a known method or result that applies, stated exactly;
- **(B)** related results only, with the precise gap;
- **(C)** nothing found in the sources searched.

List every source searched and how it was searched.
