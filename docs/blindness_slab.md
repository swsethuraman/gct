## 1. Theorem A, with proof

Notation as in `docs/washout_lemma.md` §1.  `R_r = {l·c}`, `P_r` the true
pad, `D_r^det = D_r^{det_4}`.

**Theorem A (proved).**

1. For `ell(lam) <= 4` and all `delta`: `D(lam, delta) <= 0`.
2. For `ell(lam) <= 3` and all `delta`: `det_units(lam, delta) = 0`.
3. For `ell(lam) = 4` and `delta < e`: `det_units(lam, delta) = 0`, with
   `e >= 10` certified; hence `det_units = 0` on the whole slab
   `{ell <= 4, delta <= 9}` unconditionally, and on `{ell <= 4, delta <=
   e − 1}` given the adopted value of `e`.
4. On that slab `D(lam, delta) = −pad_units(lam, delta)`, and
   `pad_units(lam, delta) = a(lam, delta) − mult_lam C[R_k]_delta`,
   `k = ell(lam)`, is a quantity about the reducible locus only
   (`docs/washout_lemma.md`, Theorem 3).

*Proof.*  (1) By the restriction lemma a weight of length `k <= 4` sees
both varieties through `D_k`, and `D_k^pad = R_k ⊆ D_k^det` for `k <= 4`
(the s27 containment theorem, `docs/n4_gate.md` §1: `l·det_3 M =
det_4 diag(l, M)` and 4-ary cubics are `3x3`-determinantal; for `k <= 3`
even `D_k^det = Sym^4 C^k`).  Containment gives `mult_pad <= mult_det`.
(2) `D_3^det = Sym^4 C^3` (rank 15 of 15, Corollary 7 of
`docs/washout_lemma.md`, now unconditional), so `I(D_3^det) = 0` and
`mult_det = a` at every length-`<= 3` weight and degree.  (3)
`D_4^det ⊂ Sym^4 C^4` is irreducible (image of an irreducible variety) of
dimension exactly 34 (sandwich, Corollary 7), hence a hypersurface; its
ideal in the UFD `C[Sym^4 C^4]` is principal, generated by an irreducible
`g` spanning a `GL_4`-stable line, i.e. a semi-invariant of weight
`(e^4)`, `e = deg g`.  So `I(D_4^det)_delta = 0` for `delta < e`, in every
weight.  `e >= 10`: the rectangular rungs `delta = 4, 6, 7, 8` carry
`mult = a` (s33, certified both primes) and `a((9^4), 9) = 0`, while the
remaining rungs `<= 9` have `a = 0`; a generator of degree `<= 9` would
be a rectangular semi-invariant of that degree vanishing on `D_4^det`,
contradicting one of these.  (4) is (2)–(3) with Theorem 3 of the washout
lemma.  ∎

**Remark.**  Item (1) does not need principality at all — it is the s27
containment — so `D <= 0` on `ell <= 4` holds at *every* degree,
including `delta >= e`.  Principality is what makes the det side
*ambient* (not merely dominant) below `e`, which is what turns a pad-side
equation into a strict cell with an exact value of `D`.

## 4. The `lam_5 = 1` sub-slab: where the restriction argument stops

**Lemma C (jet reduction; proved).**  Let `lam` have `ell(lam) = 5` and
`lam_5 = 1`.  Every highest-weight vector `h` of weight `lam` in
`C[Sym^4 C^5]_delta` has `x_5`-content 1, so each monomial of `h` contains
exactly one coefficient `c_{beta + e_5}` and otherwise coefficients
supported on `x_1..x_4`.  Hence `h(F) = H(F_0, G)` with

    F_0 = F|_{x_5 = 0} ∈ Sym^4 C^4,     G = ∂F/∂x_5 |_{x_5 = 0} ∈ Sym^3 C^4,

`H` bihomogeneous of bidegree `(delta − 1, 1)`, a `GL_4`-highest-weight
vector of weight `(lam_1, .., lam_4)`.  Writing `Pi_1^f =
closure{ jet(F) : F ∈ D_5^f } ⊆ Sym^4 C^4 x Sym^3 C^4` (the first-order
jet variety), `h ∈ I(D_5^f)` iff `H ∈ I(Pi_1^f)`, so
`mult_f(lam, delta) = mult_lam' C[Pi_1^f]_{(delta−1, 1)}`.  Consequently
**if `Pi_1^pad ⊆ Pi_1^det` then `D(lam, delta) <= 0` for every `lam` with
`lam_5 = 1`, at every `delta`.**  ∎

**The two jet varieties (proved).**  Pad: `F = l·c` with `l = l_0 + a x_5`
and `c = c_0 + x_5 q + O(x_5^2)`, `c` a generic 5-ary cubic (washout), so

    Pi_1^pad = closure{ ( l_0 c_0 ,  a c_0 + l_0 q ) : l_0 ∈ V^*, c_0 ∈ Sym^3, a ∈ C, q ∈ Sym^2 },   dim = 4 + 20 + 1 + 10 − 1 = 34,

and over a fixed `F_0 = l_0 c_0` the pad jets form the linear space
`W = C c_0 + l_0 · Sym^2 C^4` of dimension 11.  Det:
`F = det(M_0(s) + x_5 A)` gives `F_0 = det M_0`, `G = tr(adj M_0(s) · A)`;
as `A` ranges over `M_4` the `G`'s span `V_{M_0} := span(entries of adj
M_0)` (dimension 16 for generic `M_0`, measured), so `dim Pi_1^det = 50`
and `Pi_1^det = closure ∪_{M_0} {det M_0} x V_{M_0}`.

**Where the block trick stops (proved + measured).**  The s27 mechanism
represents `F_0 = l_0 c_0` as `det diag(l_0, M)`, `c_0 = det_3 M`.  Then
`adj M_0 = diag(c_0, l_0 · adj M)`, so `V_{M_0} = C c_0 + l_0 · Q_M` with
`Q_M = span(entries of adj M)` a **9**-dimensional space of quadrics: the
block representation reaches a hyperplane of `W` and misses exactly one
quadric direction (`analysis/wk9_s37_jet.py`: `dim V_{M_0} = 10`,
`rank[V_{M_0}; W] = 11`, both primes).  The 72 representations of a smooth
cubic surface give 72 hyperplanes, not all of `W`.

**Where every actual representation stops (measured, S-Z certified).**
More generally, by s32's Theorem 4 every reducible determinantal quartic
`s_1 · c` comes from one of four strata of singular subspaces
(`docs/singular_spaces.md`).  On each stratum the map `params -> (c_0, q)
= (c|_{s_5=0}, ∂_5 c|_{s_5=0})` into the 30-dimensional jet space of
cubics has Jacobian rank **29 of 30** — on the two compression branches
whose cubic families are 31-dimensional as much as on the common-kernel
branch whose cubics are `3x3`-determinantal — and adding the one
jet-preserving shear not already inside the branch (`s_1 -> s_1 + a s_5`,
which shifts `q` by `a · ∂c_0/∂s_1`) does not raise it
(`analysis/wk9_s37_jetbranch.py`, three points, exact over `Q` and both primes, box `±10^6`; S-Z certificate in §6).
The exceptional strata have cubic families of dimension 27 and 25 and
cannot reach 30.  Therefore the first-order jets of *all* reducible
determinantal pencils form a subvariety of `Pi_1^pad` of dimension at most
`4 + 29 + 1 − 1 = 33 < 34`: **no actual determinantal representation of
`l·c`, on any stratum, produces a generic pad jet.**  The universal missing
direction is a hypersurface condition on `(c_0, q)` whose tangent
functional mixes the `c_0`- and `q`-coordinates (the fibre direction over
`c_0` is 9-dimensional on every branch); its equation is not identified
here.  This is Theorem 5's stopping point, transported to first order.

**Where the border argument stops, so far (measured, both primes).**  The
containment could still hold through *limits*: over `F_0 = l_0 c_0` the
closure fibre of `Pi_1^det` contains every limit 16-plane
`L_B = lim_{t->0} V_{M_0 + tB}`.  For the block `M_0` and random linear
`B` (three per prime), `L_B ⊇ V_{M_0}` but `rank[L_B; W] = 17`, i.e.
`L_B ∩ W = V_{M_0}`: the generic first-order degeneration does not recover
the missing quadric.  Higher-order or non-generic degenerations, and
degenerations from non-block representations, are untested.

**Conclusion for Direction 5.**  The `lam_5 = 1` sub-slab is *not* closed
by restriction.  What is proved is sharper than "Theorem 5 poisons it":
the pad-jet variety strictly contains the jets of all reducible
determinantal pencils (34 versus `<= 33`), so `D <= 0` there can only come
from the boundary of `Pi_1^det`, and the first natural boundary test is
negative.  Equivalently, `lam_5 = 1` weights are the first place where a
*border* phenomenon — det-side ideal without pad-side ideal — is
structurally possible at `ell = 5`; they are also the cheapest `ell = 5`
cells (`(14,5,2,2,1)` etc.), which is why s34's `delta = 7` row is the
right next measurement and why `docs/dip_transfer.md` §5 lists them.
If instead `Pi_1^pad ⊆ closure(Pi_1^det)` is eventually proved by an
explicit degeneration, Theorem A extends to `{lam_5 = 1}` at every degree
with no further computation.  Label: **open**, with both the positive and
the negative route named.

## 5. The degree-`3d−5` defect statement, pinned

The s35 cap (`docs/theory_directions.md` §C(ii)(c)) needs: for a nodal
quartic threefold `F = 0` in `P^4` with defect `>= 1`, the Milnor algebra
`M(F) = S/J_F` has `dim M(F)_7 >= 31 = 30 + 1`.  The literature supplies
it in two adopted pieces plus one page of bookkeeping.

**Adopted 1 (Dimca, *Syzygies of Jacobian ideals and defects of linear
systems*, Bull. Math. Soc. Sci. Math. Roumanie 56 (2013), arXiv:1210.1795,
Theorem 3.1; restated in Dimca, *On the syzygies and Hodge theory of nodal
hypersurfaces*, Ann. Univ. Ferrara 63 (2017), arXiv:1310.5344, eq. (1.3)).**
For `D : f = 0` a hypersurface of degree `d` in `P^n` with isolated
singularities, with `K^*(f)` the Koszul complex of `f_0, .., f_n` graded
by `|x_j| = |dx_j| = 1` (so that a syzygy `sum a_j f_j = 0` with
`a_j ∈ S_r` lives in `H^n(K^*(f))_{n + r}`):

    dim H^n(K^*(f))_{nd − n − 1 − k}  =  def_k Σ_f      for  0 <= k <= nd − 2n − 1,

where `def_k Σ_f = τ(D) − dim S_k / Ĵ_k` is the failure of the singular
subscheme to impose independent conditions on degree-`k` forms (`Ĵ` the
saturation of `J_f`; for nodes, `τ = |N|` and `def_k = h^0(I_N(k)) −
(h^0(O(k)) − |N|)`).

**Adopted 2 (Clemens 1983; Werner 1987; Cynk, *Defect of a nodal
hypersurface*, Manuscripta Math. 104 (2001)).**  For a nodal hypersurface
`X ⊂ P^4` of degree `d` with node set `N`, the defect `δ(X) = b_4(X) −
b_2(X)` equals `def_{2d−5}(N)`, and `X` is `Q`-factorial iff `δ(X) = 0`.
(This is the factoriality criterion the s35 review verified "to the
statement quoted"; at `d = 4`, `2d − 5 = 3`: cubics through the nodes.)

**Bookkeeping (proved).**  For isolated singularities the partials
`f_0..f_4` have a one-dimensional common zero set in `C^5`, so the Koszul
cohomology `H^j(K^*(f))` vanishes for `j < n = 4` (grade of `J_f` is 4).
The degree-preserving complex ending at `(Ω^5)_m` (the map `df ∧` raises
degree by `d`) therefore has only `H^4` and `H^5`, and `H^5(K^*)_m =
M(f)_{m−5}`; its Euler characteristic depends on `(n, d)` only and equals
`dim M(f_s)_{m−5}` for smooth `f_s` (where `H^4 = 0` too).  Hence, for any
`f` with isolated singularities and any `k`,

    dim M(f)_k  =  dim M(f_s)_k  +  dim H^4(K^*(f))_{k + 5 − d}.

Put `k = 3d − 5`: the correction is `dim H^4(K^*(f))_{2d}`, and Adopted 1
with `nd − n − 1 − k' = 4d − 5 − k' = 2d` gives `k' = 2d − 5`, in range
(`2d − 5 <= 4d − 9` iff `d >= 2`).  So:

> **Theorem D (adopted 1 + adopted 2 + bookkeeping).**  For a nodal
> hypersurface `f = 0` of degree `d` in `P^4`,
> `dim (S/J_f)_{3d−5} = dim (S/J_{f_s})_{3d−5} + def_{2d−5}(N) = (smooth
> value) + δ(X)`.  At `d = 4`: `dim M(f)_7 = 30 + δ`, and the extra
> `δ` dimensions are exactly the non-Koszul syzygies among the five partials
> with coefficients of degree 4 (`H^4(K^*(f))_8`, `r = 4`).

This is precisely s35's measurement (`31` at four det pencils, `30` at
`k <= 13` general nodes) and s35's guess that the excess is "one extra
syzygy in degree 7 with degree-4 coefficients": both are now the theorem's
content, and the syzygy is `def_3(N)`-many-dimensional by construction.
The s35 cap now reads:

> **Corollary E.**  `onset I(D_5^{det_4}) <= 300`, provided (i) a general
> determinantal quartic threefold has exactly 20 nodes and no other
> singularities — Kleiman's transversality theorem for the general
> `P^4 ⊂ P^15` against the rank-`<= 2` locus (codimension 4, degree 20;
> the rank-`<= 1` locus has codimension 9 and is missed), with the nodes
> ordinary because the Hessian rank is 4 at constructed rank-2 points (s35,
> an open condition on an irreducible parameter space) — and (ii) the 16
> `3x3` minors of `M(s)` are independent cubics (s35, measured at four
> pencils, both primes; an open condition).  Then `def_3(N) >= 16 − (35 −
> 20) = 1` on a dense open subset of `D_5^{det_4}`, `dim M(F)_7 >= 31`
> there by Theorem D, the `300 x 300` minors of the degree-7 Macaulay
> matrix (generic corank 30, a nonzero polynomial) vanish on a dense subset
> hence on all of `D_5^{det_4}`, and they have degree 300 in the
> coefficients of `F`.

Labels: Adopted 1 and 2 as stated (read this session to the statements
quoted; Dimca's Theorem 3.1 via arXiv:1210.1795, the grading convention
via arXiv:1310.5344 §1; Cynk via the s35 verification); the bookkeeping
and Corollary E's logic **proved**; Kleiman's theorem **adopted**
(standard, characteristic 0); the two open conditions **measured** and
promoted to generic by irreducibility.  The cap is no longer "expectation
with 4-pencil evidence"; it is a theorem resting on named literature.
What remains expectation is only s35's *sub*-300 hope.
