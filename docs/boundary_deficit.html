The Boundary Deficit

Swami & Claude · every numerical claim machine-checked in session · novelty claims adversarially reviewed and mostly executed

This note records the end state of a dialogue that began as a dream — anabelianize GCT — and was then reviewed adversarially, idea by idea, against the literature and against direct computation. Six proposed invariant-classes turned out to be named, load-bearing structures in the field; one central identity turned out to be false; and what remains is a program-level alignment, one small computable object with actual data attached, and three questions. The deficit table in §4 is, as far as we can tell, the only artifact of the weekend that did not previously exist.

§2The corrected dictionary

The alignment that survives is program-to-program, not invariant-to-invariant. For symmetry-characterized points with reductive stabilizer, the abelian layer — multiplicity data — is information-complete (Peter–Weyl: orbit multiplicities are stabilizer-invariant dimensions, pure group data) and access-hard (occurrence, its cheapest shadow, provably fails at superpolynomial padding; the rest sits behind #P- and NP-hard walls). Complete the way a hash determines its input: faithful and unusable.

Anabelian geometry hit the same wall and renamed itself around it: determination theorems (Neukirch–Uchida, the Isom-results) were refounded by Mochizuki as mono-anabelian reconstruction — algorithms, not mere determination — because a group that determines a curve without an effective dictionary is not usable. Mulmuley's flip — positivity, explicit rules, constructible obstructions — is the same turn, made earlier, for the same reason.

mono-anabelian reconstruction : anabelian geometry :: the flip : GCT

Shared lesson, theorem-shaped on both sides: completeness does not feed you. The open front is not information but effective access — and all deficit of access is manufactured by the boundary.

§3The object

Let v be a form characterized by its symmetries with finite stabilizer H ⊂ G = GL, let Ω = G·v be the orbit-closure cone, and suppose the boundary Ω \ G·v is cut set-theoretically by a semiinvariant Δ of degree e and weight detw. Then (all standard; the packaging is the anabelian residue):

Orbit. multλ ℂ[G·v] = dim SλH — group data, computable by character averaging.

Ray. Multiplication by Δ embeds ℂ[Ω]δ-isotypic pieces into ℂ[Ω]δ+e ones; along the ray (λ + m·w·1, δ + m·e) the multiplicity is nondecreasing and stabilizes at the orbit value, since ℂ[G·v] = ℂ[Ω][1/Δ].

def(λ, δ) = dim SλH − multλ ℂ[Ω]δ ≥ 0

The boundary deficit: the λ-piece of the module ℂ[Ω][1/Δ] / ℂ[Ω], supported on the boundary. The conductor c(λ, δ) is the number of Δ-multiplications needed to stabilize — the pole order of the missing functions along the boundary. Conductor 0 means the closure multiplicity is already pure group theory.

Everything obstruction-theoretic in GCT lives in this gap: the orbit side is known (this is Landsberg's optimism, and the engine of Ikenmeyer–Kandasamy), the closure side is the mystery, and the deficit is their difference, filtered by pole order. So we computed it, in the two smallest worlds where every ingredient is classical.

§5What it leaves on the table

Q1 — A conductor formula Ogg–Shafarevich shape

Is there an effective formula for def(λ, δ) and c(λ, δ) as a sum of local contributions over boundary strata — stabilizers plus conormal weights — in the spirit of conductor-discriminant formulas? The empirical law of World A (deficit ⇒ zero-weight vector for the boundary stabilizer) is its first-order shadow, and the module-theoretic frame (deficit = graded pieces of ℂ[Ω][1/Δ]/ℂ[Ω], a local-cohomology-style object) is where such a formula would live.

Risk: the machinery is standard commutative algebra, and pieces are surely implicit in the border-apolarity and Ikenmeyer–Kandasamy toolkits; the weekend's pattern says assume so until checked.

Q2 — A discriminant for the determinant ANSWERED for n = 3 (week 3, session 6)

The ray method needs a boundary equation. Does ∂Ωdetn carry a semiinvariant equation — a "discriminant of determinantal complexity"? For n = 3, yes: Φ₁₈(det₃) = −2¹⁶3⁷5³7² ≠ 0, so the unique degree-18 invariant cuts a nonempty union of the two boundary components (session 6) — in fact exactly the boundary (session 7) — and its first conductor is now measured: c((2,2,2), 2) = 1 (session 11). For general n even the components are unknown. If no such Δ exists there, the deficit must be filtered along the full boundary ideal instead — itself a structured answer.

Risk: boundary-of-det is an active, hard corner of the field; this question may be equivalent to open problems already posed there.

Q3 — Conductor growth as the access wall the sharp one

In power-sum worlds the conductor stays tiny. Conjecture-shaped question: for det-type orbit closures, the conductor of the weights relevant to padded permanent grows with n — and superpolynomial multiplicity obstructions are exactly as hard as bounding it. Conductor-0 weights have group-computable closure multiplicities (the accessible sector); barrier results of BIP type should reappear as conductor-growth statements. This does not route around the difficulty — it proposes to measure where access dies, in small cases, as a number. The first such number now exists: the determinant's access wall at its first deficit weight is exactly one discriminant-multiplication deep.

Risk: this is a packaging of known hardness, not a solvent for it. Its value is that every term in it is computable in calibration cases (dc(perm₃) = 7 is known), so the packaging is falsifiable.
