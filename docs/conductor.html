Conductors of Orbit Closures

Swami & Claude — a working paper of the anabelian↔GCT programme. Companion log with all tables and session records: The Boundary Deficit. Code, inputs, and results: the gct repository.

Abstract. For a form v characterized by its symmetries, with orbit closure Ω, the deficit def(λ, δ) = dim SλH − multλ ℂ[Ω]δ measures the gap between the stabilizer's Peter–Weyl promise and the closure's coordinate ring, and the conductor c(λ, δ) is the pole-order depth of that gap along the boundary. We solve the two smallest power-sum worlds. For x⁴ + y⁴ (σ₂ of binary quartics): deficit = conductor = ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋⁺, in closed form, with the full stratified tower ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋⁺ → [b=1] → 0. For the Fermat cubic (the Aronhold hypersurface): the conductor equals ⌊(λ₁−2λ₃)/6⌋ on every deficit-positive weight computed (254/254), with the same boundary-geometric meaning — maximal boundary-torus weight over normal weight — while the deficit is a fat lattice count governed by a recursion down the orbit stratification. The ray-stabilization phenomenon underlying all of this is due to Ikenmeyer–Kandasamy (Lemma 5.2); our contribution is its exact per-weight minimal value and the boundary geometry that computes it. New in week 3: the fundamental invariant of the 3×3 determinant has degree e(det₃) = 18 = 2n² — not the n² of Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer's question, which we answer for n = 3 — with Φ₁₈(det₃) = −2¹⁶3⁷5³7² ≠ 0 computed exactly; its zero set inside the orbit closure is a nonempty union of the two boundary components: a discriminant for determinantal complexity. And the first conductor of a determinant is now computed, not predicted: c((2,2,2), 2) = 1 — the banked 36-subproblem evaluation returned 1,152,144,000 ≠ 0, so divisorial transport rules at the first deficit weight and the non-normal component's pathology is not yet arithmetically active there.

1Setup

G = GL(V) acts on W = SymdV*; v ∈ W is characterized by its symmetries with finite stabilizer H; Ω = G·v is the affine orbit-closure cone. Peter–Weyl gives multλ ℂ[G·v] = dim (Sλ*)H =: m(λ). When the boundary is cut set-theoretically by a semiinvariant Δ of degree e and weight detw, multiplication by Δ makes multλ ℂ[Ω]δ nondecreasing along the ray (λ + mw·1, δ + me), stabilizing at m(λ) since ℂ[G·v] = ℂ[Ω][1/Δ]. Define def(λ, δ) = m(λ) − multλ ℂ[Ω]δ ≥ 0 and the conductor c(λ, δ) = the stabilization index — the maximal pole order along the boundary among functions in the λ-isotypic. Stabilization at an explicit uniform index is Ikenmeyer–Kandasamy's Lemma 5.2 (for power sums with #variables ≥ degree, by tableau methods, without optimality analysis); everything below concerns the exact minimal index and its geometry.

2World A: σ₂(v₄), solved

W = binary quartics, v = x⁴ + y⁴, H = μ₄²⋊S₂ (order 32), Ω = the catalecticant cubic hypersurface {J = 0}. Since disc = 256(I³ − 27J²) identically, disc|Ω = 256·I³: the boundary divisor (the tangent-developable cone) is cut by the quadratic invariant I, along which the discriminant ramifies with index 3; the correct ray steps by I (degree 2, weight det⁴). The boundary's dense orbit G·x³y has stabilizer the torus diag(t, t⁻³); the boundary point is a smooth point of Ω, with normal weight of size 8.

Theorem A. For every weight (a, b), a + b = 4δ: def = c = max(0, ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋); equivalently mult(a,b) ℂ[Ω]δ = N(a,b) − ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋⁺, where N counts unordered pairs {p, q}, p + q = a − b, p ≡ q ≡ −b (mod 4), excluding p = q with b odd. Corollaries: support law (def > 0 ⇔ b ≤ δ−2), max conductor ⌊δ/2⌋, total deficit ⌊δ²/4⌋ for all δ. verified δ ≤ 60; proofs below

Pole law. The Sylvester model: on the transversal family fs = x³y + s·xy³ (which lies in {J = 0} identically, with I(fs) = −s/4), the Waring pair is c = ±1/(8√s), ℓ = x ± √s·y. The isotypic is spanned (FFT + Peter–Weyl; the model's count matches the group average on the full grid including the polar sector) by classes ep,q,b = Sym[ℓ̂₁pℓ̂₂q]·(ℓ̂₁∧ℓ̂₂)b. Writing the bracket coefficient at uk as Ak(p,q)·[ιq + (−1)b+kιp] with Ak = [uk](1+u)p(1−u)q and ι⁴ = −1: the phase survives exactly when k ≡ b + (p−q)/4 (mod 2), which coincides with the integrality parity; so only k₀ ∈ {0, 1} is needed, and A₀ = 1, A₁ = p − q ≠ 0 away from the identically-zero class. Hence pole(ep,q,b) = ⌊(p+q−2b)/8⌋ = ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋, uniformly over the classes at a weight (77/77 grid).

Collapse. GL₂-torus weight spaces are one-dimensional, so all leading terms at a given pole level are proportional to a single monomial: the pole filtration climbs by at most one per level. Combined with the verified identity (all weights, δ ≤ 60), every level climbs by exactly one: deficit = conductor = the pole value. The closed forms follow; Σb ≤ δ−2 ⌊(δ−b)/2⌋ = ⌊δ²/4⌋ gives the total (independently proved as a rational-function identity through t²⁰⁰ with an a-priori denominator bound < 100).

The tower. ℂ[σ₂] is Cohen–Macaulay (Hankel) and deg(I)·deg(σ₂) = 6 = deg τ(v₄), so (Ī) is prime and the level-1 ring is ℂ[τ̂]. The tangent developable's normalization is ⊕δ Sym3δ ⊗ Symδ (the (ℓ,m) ↦ ℓ³m parametrization; the jet-bundle resolution of AFPRW), one copy of each (4δ−b, b), b ≤ δ; the non-normality gap is exactly one copy of S(4δ−1,1) per degree (rank-verified at δ ≤ 4: dimensions 5, 14, 29, 50; tables to δ = 30) — the equivariant globalization of k[t²,t³] ⊂ k[t] missing t along the cuspidal edge. Level 2 is the Veronese cone; the tower is ⌊(a−3b)/8⌋⁺ → [b = 1] → 0.

3World B: the transport theorem

W = ternary cubics, v = Fermat, H = μ₃³⋊S₃ (order 162), Ω = {S = 0}, the Aronhold quartic hypersurface (S constructed by exact linear algebra; 25 terms). The space of degree-12 invariants on Ω is one-dimensional, so disc|Ω = c·T² with T the canonical degree-6 semiinvariant (constructed explicitly; 103 terms; T(Fermat) = −5832). The boundary's dense orbit is the cuspidal locus G·(x²y + z³): a smooth point of Ω, stabilizer 𝔾ₘ × μ₃ with torus diag(t, t⁻², 1), normal weight −6, and ν∂(T) = 1 exactly: on the transversal line fs = x²y + s·y³ + z³ (inside {S = 0} identically), T(fs) = −864s.

Theorem B (conductor transport). On every deficit-positive weight with δ ≤ 10 (254 of 254): c(λ) = ⌊(λ₁ − 2λ₃)/6⌋ = ⌊μmax(λ)/|wN|⌋, the maximal boundary-torus weight over the normal weight.
upper bound proved; per-class law and attainment machine-verified; phase write-out routine

Block orders. On the transversal family with σ̃² = 3s, σ̃ = ρ³ (T-unit = ρ⁶): the normalized Waring blocks have exact ρ-orders ℓ̂₁, ℓ̂₂: −1; ℓ̂₃ = z: 0; the collision wedge ℓ̂₁∧ℓ̂₂: +1; mixed wedges: −1; the determinant: +1. A class with single-counts (a₁,a₂,a₃), wedge-counts (q₁₂,q₁₃,q₂₃), det-power r = λ₃ has naive pole (a₁+a₂ + q₁₃+q₂₃ − q₁₂ − r)/6, whose maximum over class types is (λ₁ − 2λ₃)/6 — the formula's linear form is the block-order linear program. Since Young symmetrization only cancels, and FFT gives spanning, c(λ) ≤ ⌊(λ₁−2λ₃)/6⌋ rigorously.

Attainment and calibration. The exact class calculus (generic contraction, exact in ρ) attains the bound at every deficit-positive weight tested — the (3δ,0,0)-row in full, five wedge/det weights, and a systematic sweep of all 82 valid weights with λ₁ ≤ 9 plus specials. The two exceptions in the sweep are precisely the empty-support weights (10,1,1) and (13,1,1): there the contraction shadow still reports pole 1, but the deficit is 0 — the Young projection kills the top. This locates the orphan phenomenon exactly: the formula computes the shadow maximum; on empty support the projection cancels it. 80/82 · 254/254

What remains. The B-analog of the A₀/A₁ phase argument — the same bookkeeping with three lines and ι³ = −1 phases — written out; the template has not failed at any of the several hundred weights it has been tested on.

4The towers

The increments of the T-ray are, by the multiplication exact sequence, multiplicities of ℂ[Ω]/(T) — the cusp cone's ring — and those are bounded by the cusp stabilizer's invariant counts (zero violations on 616 weights; evidence (T) is prime), with the shortfall being the cusp cone's own deficit: the Ogg–Shafarevich structure recurses down the stratification. World A's recursion terminates: corrections ∈ {0, 1}, supported exactly at (4δ−1, 1). World B's stays heavy: the level-2 correction absorbs 1128 of 3001 eigencount units in range, admits no floor law, and passes to the conic-with-tangent stratum — whose full stabilizer is two-dimensional and non-reductive (torus diag(1,−2,4) plus a nilpotent): the tower's first wild point, at dimensions 9 → 8 → 7 → 5 → 3. The deficit in B is thus a genuine lattice count (fat steps from GL₃ weight multiplicities), while the conductor stays a floor — the sharpest structural difference between the two worlds.

5The determinant: e(det₃) = 18, and a discriminant for determinantal complexity

Theorem (census, combinatorial). For cubic forms in nine variables, dim Symδ(Sym³ℂ⁹)SL₉ = 0 for every δ < 18, and = 1 at δ = 18. By the first fundamental theorem the invariants are spanned by complete ε₉-contractions of f⊗δ, and two vanishing laws kill everything below 18: a copy sending two legs into one ε dies (symmetric slots against antisymmetry — this alone empties δ = 3, 6, where weight < 3 forces it), and two copies carrying the same ε-triple cancel in pairs (three slot-swaps, sign (−1)³ — this empties δ = 9, 12, 15, where C(weight, 3) = 1, 4, 10 < #copies forces a repeat by pigeonhole). At δ = 18, weight 6, the 18 copies must carry 18 distinct triples among C(6,3) = 20 with the omitted pair complementary; all such choices coincide up to sign under ε-relabeling. So there is a unique candidate Φ₁₈ — and it is nonzero by the evaluation below. The plethysm census (Murnaghan–Nakayama, exact) confirms 0, 0, 0, 0, 1 independently. theorem

Theorem (the value). In the tensor-contraction normalization, Φ₁₈(det₃) = −877 879 296 000 = −2¹⁶ · 3⁷ · 5³ · 7² ≠ 0. Hence the minimal degree of a nonzero SL₉-invariant on the orbit closure Ω = GL₉·det₃ is e(det₃) = 18 = 2n², with a one-dimensional fundamental space. Method: the contraction is a signed exact-cover sum over ~10¹¹ assignments; with an order-independent parity scheme (variable- plus depositor-inversions per ε) the future of the search depends only on the six ε-masks, so the sum collapses to a level DP over ≤ 4.03 × 10⁷ states per layer — 273 seconds, against an estimated CPU-week for the tree walk. Verified by: a reversed-order DP traversing a disjoint state space (identical value); transpose-paired subproblems returning equal partials; the n = 2 anchor reproducing Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer's e(det₂) = 4 = n² case; an independent backtracking evaluator convergent on subproblems; and a 36-subproblem decomposition summing to the total. computed, quadruply cross-checked

Context. Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer (Fundamental invariants of orbit closures) prove e(detn) ≥ n² and ask when equality holds: true at n = 2, and at n = 4 by their computer verification of P4,16(det₄) ≠ 0; for odd n their degree-n² candidate is annihilated (at n = 3 the whole ambient space vanishes, by the census above), and n = 3 was left undetermined. The result here settles it: e(det₃) = 2n², the first determinant whose fundamental invariant does not live in degree n² — and 18 is exactly the first degree the contraction combinatorics permits for any cubic in nine variables, so det₃ achieves the generic minimum. prior-art pass Aug 2026

Corollary (the discriminant). On the open orbit, Φ₁₈(g · det₃) = det(g)⁶ Φ₁₈(det₃) ≠ 0; so V(Φ₁₈) ∩ Ω is a nonempty, G-stable, pure-codimension-1 subset of the boundary. By Hüttenhain–Lairez the boundary ∂Ω has exactly two irreducible components, both divisors — so V(Φ₁₈) ∩ Ω is a union of boundary components: a discriminant of determinantal complexity. Among cubics of border determinantal complexity ≤ 3, degeneracy is (at least one component of) the vanishing of one explicit degree-18 equation. Both components, it turns out: each is SL₉-unstable (P₁ has eight essential variables; P₂ is bihomogeneous of degree (2,1) for a 3+6 split, killed by the 1-PS with weights (−2,+1)), so the entire boundary lies in the null cone and V(Φ₁₈) ∩ Ω = ∂Ω exactly — confirmed by direct evaluation (value 0 at both representatives). Two structural corollaries: any two same-degree invariants on the closure are proportional (dense-orbit ratio argument), so the invariant ring is a numerical-semigroup ring, the GIT quotient a monomial curve, and its classical conductor a new invariant of det₃; and Φ₁₈(perm₃) = 2²⁰3⁴·5·7·17 ≠ 0 (same quotiented DP; ratio to the determinant −4725/272), which with the stabilizer dimensions proves perm₃ ∉ Ω(det₃) by invariant evaluation. The deficit calculus is open for business as well: def((2,2,2), 2) = 1 exactly — the first deficit of a determinant — with deficit totals 1, 6, 31, 141, 618, 2488 through δ = 7, no occurrence-style equations anywhere in that range — sharpened in session 8: the δ = 2 row is exact (0, 0, 1; no degree-2 equations), every λ′-covariant vanishes at the point det₃ by a row-counting lemma, and the deficit class is indecomposable (the whole (a,a,a,b⁶) family has ambient multiplicity 0 at δ = 4, 7, 10, 13, 16), closing the product-of-covariants shortcut. Session 9 supplied the transport analysis: Ω is smooth along generic P₁ points (the End-parametrization is submersive there, rank 65), Φ₁₈ ramifies to order exactly 6 along P₁ (equivariant slice + transverse family + the pencil identity 6 × 9 = 54, with all nine generic pencil hits landing in P₁), P₂ is invisible to End-curves — the wild, non-normal component — with m₂ = 9 derived from torus integrality, giving div(Φ₁₈) = 6P₁ + 9P₂. theorem + programme

Theorem (the first conductor of a determinant). c((2,2,2), 2) = 1. Session 10 corrected session 9's prediction (2 → 1) by separating divisorial orders from ray steps: one Φ₁₈-step clears m₁ = 6 units along P₁ and m₂ = 9 along P₂, so the divisorial transport prediction is ⌈2/6⌉ = ⌈2/9⌉ = 1, and c ≥ 2 could occur only if divisorial extension failed — precisely a failure of normality, living on P₂. The k = 1 computation therefore measured whether non-normality bites at the first deficit weight: zero meant the wild component's pathology is arithmetically active from the very first class; nonzero meant the divisorial calculus rules and c = 1. The banked 36-subproblem evaluation is complete and the value is nonzero: the λ′ = (8,8,8,6⁶), δ = 20 highest-weight pairing at a balanced-substitution orbit point evaluates to 2 × 576,072,000 = 1,152,144,000. The 36 subproblems reduce to 18 orbit pairs under the point's signed symmetry (validated empirically: the duplicated pair returned identical values, 108,712,800, from independent multi-billion-state runs); all 19 runs ended in the mandatory single final state; the 19 values obey V = W(σ₆⁻¹σ₇) with eight of them landing as blind predictions; every sign tracks sgn(σ₆)sgn(σ₇). Hence the k = 1 slot is filled, divisorial transport rules on its first test on a determinant, and the first conductor of a determinant equals 1 — the access wall sits at its floor at (2,2,2). computed 2026-08-24; full table in the companion log

The nursery's tame/wild dichotomy reappears verbatim one level up: P₁ tame and provable, P₂ wild and formula-dependent — but at the first deficit weight the wild component's arithmetic stayed quiet. Whether it wakes at deeper weights (the k = 2 rung, the fat (3δ−4,2,2) family) is the standing question.

7Open problems, refreshed

(1) The determinant ray, upward. The first conductor is 1; stabilize the deficit along the Φ₁₈-ray beyond k = 1 (the k = 2 rung of (2,2,2), then the fat (3δ−4,2,2) family) and find the first weight, if any, where non-normality bites — the wild component's arithmetic debut. A second balanced-substitution point as an independent check of the k = 1 certificate is banked and cheap. (2) The exact level-2 law in World B: increments as invariants of the non-reductive conic-tangent stabilizer (unipotent invariants), giving the full O–S sum for the deficit. (3) Optimality gap: characterize the empty-support (orphan) weights where the shadow maximum is killed by the Young projection. (4) The det-world's numerical semigroup: does the degree-24 census admit new invariants (S beyond 18), deciding the singularity of the monomial GIT curve. (5) Whether the conductor formula ⌊μmax/wN⌋ holds for all symmetry-characterized forms whose boundary has a dense orbit with a smooth point and a one-dimensional stabilizer torus — the natural general conjecture of this paper.
