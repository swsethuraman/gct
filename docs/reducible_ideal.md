# The ideal of the reducible locus: the (★) criterion as a theorem, its corollaries, and where `I(R_r)` begins

Session 40 (2026-09-02), branch `s40-onset`.  Session 36's criterion (★)
(`docs/stabiliser_reduction.md` §4.2, derived there post hoc and used at 91
cells) written as a general theorem with the Bruhat proof in full, for any
`n`, `r`, `δ`, and for any padding exponent `k`; the corollaries the

## 0. Verdict

> **Theorem 1 (★).**  Let `V = C^r`, `W = Sym^n V^*`, `G = GL(V)`, and for
> `1 ≤ k ≤ n` let `X^{(k)} = {l^k · c : l ∈ V^*, c ∈ Sym^{n−k} V^*} ⊆ W` be
> the locus of forms with a `k`-fold linear factor (`k = 1`: the reducible
> locus `R_r = X^{(1)}`).  A highest-weight vector `v` of weight `λ` in
> `C[W]_δ` vanishes on `X^{(k)}` if and only if every monomial
> `c_{α^1} ⋯ c_{α^δ}` of `v` has, for every `i ∈ {1, .., r}`, a factor
> `c_{α^j}` with `α^j_i ≤ k − 1`.
>
> **Corollary A (point-free multiplicity).**  `mult_λ C[X^{(k)}]_δ = a(λ,δ)
> − dim( HWV_λ(C[W]_δ) ∩ span M_★^{(k)} )`, with `M_★^{(k)}` the set of
> weight-`λ` monomials satisfying the condition — a linear-algebra
> computation on the highest-weight kernel, with no evaluation points.
>
> **Corollary B (Kadish–Landsberg's padding bound).**  If `λ_1 < kδ` then
> every highest-weight vector of weight `λ` in `C[W]_δ` vanishes on `X^{(k)}`,
> so `mult_λ C[Y]_δ = 0` for every `G`-stable closed `Y ⊆ X^{(k)}`, in
> particular for the orbit closure of any padded form `l^k P`.
>
> **Corollary C (invariants).**  Every `SL_r`-invariant of `r`-ary `n`-ics
> vanishes on `X^{(k)}` as soon as `n < kr`; for `k = 1`, every invariant of
> `r`-ary `n`-ics with `r > n` vanishes on the reducible locus.
>
> **Onsets for quartics (proved + measured).**  `I(R_r)` begins in degree
> **5** for every `r ≥ 5`, with the unique degree-5 invariant `I_5` of
> quinary quartics (weight `(4^5)`), and `I(R_r)_5` is exactly the isotypic
> component of `(4^5, 0^{r−5})`; for `r = 3, 4` it begins in degree 6 with
> the ternary-quartic combination of weight `(8,8,8)`.  The *length-6* part
> of `I(R_6)` begins in degree **6** with `I_6` (weight `(4^6)`).  Session
> 36's statement "`I(D_6^{pad})` begins at `δ = 6`" is to be read as this
> length-6 statement: the ideal as a whole contains the length-5 image of
> `I_5` already in degree 5.  Generators known: `I_5` (5); `I_6` (6);
> `(9,9,8,1,1)`, `(8,8,8,2,2)` at `r = 5` and `(10,8,7,1,1,1)` at `r = 6`, all
> of degree 7 and none in the ideal generated by `I_5`, `I_6`.
>
> **Literature (§4).**  The mechanism of Corollary B — highest-weight
> vectors evaluated at `x_1^{k} h`, controlled by their monomial support —
> is Kadish–Landsberg's coordinate proof of their Theorem 1.3.  The exact
> "if and only if" criterion for the full locus and its use as a point-free
> multiplicity formula we did not find stated in the sources checked
> (Kadish–Landsberg 2014, Chipalkatti's Brill-type and coincident-root loci,
> CGGHMNS 2019 on secants of reducible hypersurfaces, Landsberg's book); we
> claim them as not-found-in-the-literature, not as new.

## 1. Setting

`V = C^r` with basis `e_1..e_r`, dual basis `x_1..x_r` of `V^*`; `W = Sym^n
V^*` the forms of degree `n` in `x_1..x_r`; `G = GL(V)` acting on `W` by
substitution and on `C[W]` by `(g·f)(F) = f(g^{−1}·F)`.  `C[W]` is the
polynomial ring in the coefficient functionals `c_α`, `α ∈ N^r`, `|α| = n`
(the programme's normalisation `c_α = e^α/α!` is immaterial here: any
rescaling of the `c_α` by nonzero scalars leaves every statement below
unchanged).  `T ⊂ G` the diagonal torus; `c_α` is a `T`-weight vector of
weight `α`, a monomial `∏_j c_{α^j}` has weight `Σ_j α^j`.

**Highest-weight vectors.**  Fix the Borel subgroup `B = T·U ⊂ G` whose
unipotent radical `U` is generated by the root groups of the simple raising
operators of the programme's convention (`E_{ij}`, `i < j`, acting on the
`c_α` by `E_{ij} c_α = (α_i + 1) c_{α + e_i − e_j}`, `docs/isotypic_rank.md`,
`analysis/wk8_s30_core.py`).  A highest-weight vector of weight `λ` in
`C[W]_δ` is a `T`-eigenvector of weight `λ` killed by every `E_{ij}`, `i <
j`; equivalently, a `B`-eigenvector: `b·v = χ(b) v` for all `b ∈ B`, `χ` the
character extending `λ`.  Its weight `λ` is dominant, `λ_1 ≥ ... ≥ λ_r ≥ 0`,
with `|λ| = nδ`, and `ℓ(λ) ≤ δ` (each monomial has `δ` factors, and
dominance forces every row of `λ` to be hit by some factor).  Write
`HWV_λ(C[W]_δ)` for the space of such vectors, `a(λ, δ)` its dimension
(the plethysm multiplicity).

**The two families of subspaces.**  For `1 ≤ k ≤ n` and `i ∈ [r]`,

    L_i^{(k)} = { F ∈ W : x_i^k divides F } = span{ x^α : α_i ≥ k } = { F : c_α(F) = 0 for all α with α_i < k },

a `T`-stable coordinate subspace, and `X^{(k)} = ∪_{l ≠ 0} l^k · Sym^{n−k}
V^* = G · L_i^{(k)}` for any `i` (`G` is transitive on nonzero linear
forms; `g·(x_i^k c) = (g·x_i)^k (g·c)`), together with `0`.  `X^{(k)}` is
closed (image of the proper map `P(V^*) × P(Sym^{n−k}V^*) → P(W)`, plus the
origin).  `k = 1` gives the reducible-with-a-linear-factor locus, the
programme's `R_r` at `n = 4`; `k = n − m` gives the locus containing the
orbit closure of any padded form `l^{n−m} P`, `deg P = m`.

**Lemma 1 (ideal of a coordinate subspace; standard).**  `I(L_i^{(k)}) =
(c_α : α_i < k)`, and a polynomial lies in this ideal iff each of its
monomials is divisible by some `c_α` with `α_i < k`.

*Proof.*  `L_i^{(k)}` is a linear subspace, so its ideal is generated by the
linear forms vanishing on it, which are exactly the span of the `c_α` with
`α_i < k` (the `c_α` with `α_i ≥ k` restrict to coordinates on `L_i^{(k)}`).
An ideal generated by a set of variables is a monomial ideal, and a
polynomial lies in it iff every monomial does.  ∎

**Lemma 2 (`B`-stable zero sets).**  If `v` is a `B`-eigenvector in `C[W]`
then its zero set `Z(v) = {F : v(F) = 0}` is `B`-stable.

*Proof.*  `v(b^{−1} F) = (b·v)(F) = χ(b) v(F)`, and `χ(b) ≠ 0`.  ∎

**Lemma 3 (Bruhat).**  There is an index `i_0` with `B ⊆ P := Stab_G(C·x_{i_0})`,
`P` a maximal parabolic subgroup; the Weyl group `W_G = S_r` acts on `V^*`
by permutation matrices, `w · L_i^{(k)} = L_{w(i)}^{(k)}`; the stabiliser of
`i_0` in `S_r` is `W_P = Stab_{S_r}(i_0)`; and

    G = ⊔_{w ∈ S_r / W_P} B w P        (Bruhat decomposition, one cell per coset, i.e. per index w(i_0) ∈ [r]).

*Proof.*  `B` stabilises a complete flag of `V^*`; its first step is a
`B`-stable line, hence a `T`-stable line, hence a coordinate line `C·x_{i_0}`
(the `x_i` have distinct `T`-weights).  So `B ⊆ P = Stab_G(C x_{i_0})`, the
stabiliser of a line in `V^*`, which is a maximal parabolic containing `B`.
The Bruhat decomposition `G = ⊔_{w ∈ W_G/W_P} B w P` for a parabolic `P ⊇ B`
is standard (e.g. Borel, *Linear algebraic groups*, §21; Humphreys §28), with
`W_P` the Weyl group of the Levi of `P`, here the permutations fixing `i_0`.
Permutation matrices act on the coordinates by `w·x_i = x_{w(i)}` (up to the
convention of the dual action, which permutes indices by `w` or `w^{−1}`;
either way the set `{w·L_{i_0}^{(k)}}` over a set of coset representatives is
`{L_1^{(k)}, .., L_r^{(k)}}`).  ∎

## 2. Theorem 1 and its proof *(proved)*

**Theorem 1 (★, for any `n, r, δ, k`).**  Let `v ∈ HWV_λ(C[W]_δ)` (or, more
generally, any `B`-eigenvector of `C[W]`).  Then

    v ∈ I(X^{(k)})   ⟺   v ∈ I(L_i^{(k)}) for every i ∈ [r]
                     ⟺   every monomial of v has, for every i, a factor c_α with α_i ≤ k − 1.

*Proof.*  Since `P` stabilises the line `C x_{i_0}`, it stabilises
`L_{i_0}^{(k)} = x_{i_0}^k · Sym^{n−k} V^*`.  Hence, by Lemma 3,

    X^{(k)} = G · L_{i_0}^{(k)} = ∪_{w} B w P · L_{i_0}^{(k)} = ∪_w B · (w L_{i_0}^{(k)}) = ∪_{i=1}^{r} B · L_i^{(k)}.

If `v` vanishes on `X^{(k)}` it vanishes on each `L_i^{(k)} ⊆ X^{(k)}`.
Conversely, if `v` vanishes on `L_i^{(k)}` then, `Z(v)` being `B`-stable
(Lemma 2), `v` vanishes on `B · L_i^{(k)}`; doing this for every `i` gives
vanishing on `X^{(k)}`.  The second equivalence is Lemma 1.  ∎

Two remarks.  (i) The criterion does not depend on which Borel `B ⊇ T` is
used to define highest-weight vectors, nor on the ordering convention that
makes `E_{ij}` "raising": every `i` enters symmetrically.  (ii) Nothing is
special to the linear factor being `x_{i_0}`: for a `G`-stable closed `X =
G·L` with `L` a coordinate subspace stabilised by a parabolic `P ⊇ B`, the
same proof gives `v ∈ I(X) ⟺ v ∈ ∩_{w} I(w L)`, a monomial condition — the
form in which the argument appears for Chow varieties and other
"`G`-saturations of coordinate subspaces".

*Consistency (measured, s36).*  At `n = 4`, `r = 5, 6`, `k = 1`, the
point-based `mult_pad` (evaluation at true padded-permanent points) and the
point-free `mult_red` of Corollary A agree at all 91 cells of
`results/s36_red_table.md`; at `r = 5` they must (`P_5 = R_5`), and at
`r = 6` their agreement is the measured statement that no permanent-specific
equation exists through `δ = 7` in the components reached.  The three
exhibited vectors `(8,4,4,4,4)_6`, `(9,9,8,1,1)_7`, `(10,8,7,1,1,1)_7`
satisfy (★) monomial by monomial on their exact integer supports.

## 3. Corollaries

**Corollary A (point-free multiplicity; proved).**

    mult_λ C[X^{(k)}]_δ  =  a(λ, δ) − dim( HWV_λ(C[W]_δ) ∩ span M_★^{(k)} ),

where `M_★^{(k)}` is the set of weight-`λ` degree-`δ` monomials in the `c_α`
that have, for every `i`, a factor with `α_i ≤ k−1`.  Moreover `span
M_★^{(k)}` is `Stab_W(λ)`-stable, so the intersection can be taken inside
the `χ_λ`-isotypic reduction of `docs/stabiliser_reduction.md` (that is what
`analysis/wk9_s36_red.py` computes).

*Proof.*  By the isotypic-rank lemma (paper 1, Lemma `lem:isorank`),
`mult_λ C[X]_δ = a − dim{u : Σ u_j h_j ∈ I(X)}` for a basis `h_j` of
`HWV_λ`; by Theorem 1, `Σ u_j h_j ∈ I(X^{(k)})` iff all its monomials lie in
`M_★^{(k)}`, i.e. iff `Σ u_j h_j ∈ span M_★^{(k)}`.  Permuting variable
indices within blocks of `λ` preserves the condition, so `M_★^{(k)}` is
`Stab_W(λ)`-stable.  ∎

**Corollary B (the padding bound; proved).**  If `λ_1 < kδ` then
`HWV_λ(C[W]_δ) ⊆ I(X^{(k)})`; hence `mult_λ C[Y]_δ = 0` for every `G`-stable
closed `Y ⊆ X^{(k)}` — in particular for `Y = closure(G · l^{k} P)`, the
orbit closure of any form with a `k`-fold linear factor — and no cell
`(λ, δ)` with `λ_1 < kδ` can carry a multiplicity obstruction of the form
`mult_λ C[Y]_δ > mult_λ C[Y']_δ`.

*Proof.*  A monomial of weight `λ` has `δ` factors whose `i`-th exponents
sum to `λ_i ≤ λ_1 < kδ`, so some factor has `α_i ≤ k − 1`; this holds for
every `i`, so every monomial satisfies the condition of Theorem 1, and every
`v ∈ HWV_λ` lies in `I(X^{(k)}) ⊆ I(Y)`.  With Lemma `lem:isorank`,
`mult_λ C[Y]_δ = 0`.  ∎

This is the first half of Kadish–Landsberg's Theorem 1.3 (*Padded
polynomials, their cousins, and geometric complexity theory*, Comm. Algebra
42 (2014); arXiv:1204.4693): with their notation `n − m = k`, `d = δ`, the
module `S_π W` lies in the degree-`d` ideal of the variety of forms
divisible by an `(n−m)`-th power of a linear form whenever `p_1 < d(n−m)`.
Their second half — that for `p_1 ≥ min(d(n−1), dn − m)` the module does
*not* lie in the ideal — is a different statement, about specific
highest-weight vectors not vanishing, and is not a consequence of (★)
(which decides each vector but says nothing about existence).

**Corollary C (invariants; proved).**  An `SL_r`-invariant of degree `δ` of
`r`-ary `n`-ics has weight `((nδ/r)^r)`, and `nδ/r < kδ` iff `n < kr`.  So
for `n < kr` every invariant vanishes on `X^{(k)}`; for `k = 1`, every
invariant of `r`-ary `n`-ics with `r > n` vanishes on the reducible locus.
At `n = 4`: every invariant of quinary or senary quartics vanishes on
`{l·c}` (s36's `I_5`, `I_6`).  At `n = 3`: every `SL_5`-invariant of cubic
threefolds — degrees `10, 15, ...` — vanishes on the reducible cubic
threefolds `{l · q}`.

**Corollary D (restriction and length; proved, the programme's standing
lemma restated for `X^{(k)}`).**  For `ℓ(λ) = s ≤ r`, `mult_λ C[X_r^{(k)}]_δ
= mult_λ C[X_s^{(k)}]_δ` (restriction to the first `s` variables carries
`HWV_λ` isomorphically and `X_r^{(k)}|_{C^s} = X_s^{(k)}`); so `I(X_r^{(k)})`
in degree `δ` is determined, at weights of length `s < r`, by
`I(X_s^{(k)})`, and only weights of length exactly `r` are new at `r`.

**Proposition (onsets; proved + measured as marked).**

1. For every `r ≥ 5`, `onset I(R_r) = 5`, and `I(R_r)_5` is the single
   isotypic component of weight `(4^5, 0^{r−5})`, spanned over `G` by `I_5`.
   *Proof.*  `I(R_r)_δ = 0` for `δ ≤ 4`: a degree-`δ` highest-weight vector
   has length `≤ δ ≤ 4`, and by Corollary D vanishing on `R_r` is vanishing
   on `R_s`, `s ≤ 4`, where the record is empty (rows 2–4, measured) — the
   measured half.  `I_5 ∈ I(R_5)` by Corollary C (`4 < 5`) — proved; by
   Corollary D it lifts to `I(R_r)` for `r ≥ 5` as the weight-`(4^5,0^{r−5})`
   vector (`a((4^5, 0^{r−5}), 5) = a((4^5), 5) = 1`).  The remaining

2. The length-6 part of `I(R_6)` begins in degree exactly 6, with `I_6`:

   is measured on 3 of the 4 cells (empty); `(9,6,4,2,2,1)` at `n_χ ≈ 38583`

   C[W]_2` or `I_6 · C[W]_1`.  By Littlewood–Richardson every constituent
   `ν` of `S_{(4^5,0)} ⊗ Sym^2(Sym^4 C^6)` contains `(4,4,4,4,4,0)`
   componentwise, and none of `(9,9,8,1,1,0)`, `(8,8,8,2,2,0)`,
   `(10,8,7,1,1,1)` does (a fourth part `1` or `2` against `4`); and

   minimal generators of degree 7 — **proved**
   given s36's measured `mult_red` values, which are two-sided: `mult_red ≤

## 5. Literature *(one pass, as found)*

- **Kadish–Landsberg 2014** (arXiv:1204.4693, Thm 1.3 and Prop. 1.12; read
  this session): the variety `F_{n−m}(S^n W^*)` of forms divisible by an
  `(n−m)`-th power of a linear form; `S_π W ⊂ I_d` whenever `p_1 < d(n−m)`,
  proved by a coordinate computation showing highest-weight vectors of a
  standard form vanish on `(e^1)^{n−m} h`, and again via the Foulkes–Howe
  map `S^d(S^n W) → S^{d(n−m)} W ⊗ S^d(S^m W)`; the inheritance principle for
  the number of parts.  This is Corollary B's mechanism.  The "if and only
  if" of Theorem 1 and the multiplicity formula of Corollary A are not
  stated there.
- **Chipalkatti**, *A regularity result for a locus of Brill type*
  (arXiv:math/0405236) and **Abdesselam–Chipalkatti**, *Brill–Gordan loci,
  transvectants and an analogue of the Foulkes conjecture*
  (arXiv:math/0411110): the loci of two `e`-fold hyperplanes and of
  coincident-root binary forms; regularity and degree-3 generators, by
  transvectants, hypergeometric series and Feynman diagrams.  Different
  loci, different methods; no monomial criterion.
- **Catalisano–Geramita–Gimigliano–Harbourne–Migliore–Nagel–Shin**, *Secant
  varieties of the varieties of reducible hypersurfaces in P^n*, J. Algebra
  528 (2019): dimensions of secant varieties of the loci of reducible forms
  of given splitting type; not their ideals.
- **Landsberg**, *Geometry and Complexity Theory* (2017), Ch. 8–9: the
  ideal of the Chow variety via Brill's and Gordan's equations and the
  Hadamard–Howe map; the technique "a highest-weight vector vanishes on
  `G·Y` iff it vanishes on the `B`-saturation of `Y`" is used there for the
  Chow variety and is folklore for `G`-saturations of coordinate subspaces.

**Verdict.**  The technique is standard and the automatic case is
Kadish–Landsberg's; the exact criterion for `X^{(k)}` with its point-free
multiplicity corollary and its use at 91 measured cells are, as far as one
pass shows, not on record.  If a reader knows a source, the right response
is a citation, not a defence.
