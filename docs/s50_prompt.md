## 1. Why this session exists

Every equation for the determinantal locus we can currently construct sits far
above anything we can measure:

| construction | lowest degree with a known equation |
|---|---|
| Macaulay minors, `r = 6` | 661 certified, 1148 proved |
| Macaulay minors, `r = 5`, `n = 4` | 300 |
| Macaulay minors, `r = 5`, `n = 3` (paper 1) | 65 |
| **Landsberg–Manivel–Ressayre module** | **24** |

Our measured range is `δ ≤ 9`.  LMR is the only known equation anywhere near it,
and the only one whose weight we can write down.  Get it, check it, evaluate it.

## 2. Task 1 — derive the module and its weight independently

Read arXiv:1004.4802 (Landsberg–Manivel–Ressayre, hypersurfaces with degenerate
duals) and derive the equation module and weight **from the paper**, not from our
reading of it.  Our current reading, to be confirmed or refuted:

    Ω(k,d) = (d−1)(d−2)(k+2) ω_1 + (d(k+2) − 2k − 5) ω_2 + 2 ω_{k+3},
    degree (k+2)(d−1) = 2n(n−1).

At `n = 4`: `λ = (65, 17, 2^7)`, `δ = 24`, `ℓ(λ) = 9`, `|λ| = 96 = 4·24`.

Cheap checks first:

- `|λ| = 4δ`.  Failure means the weight is wrong.
- `ℓ(λ) ≤ 10`.  Concision gives `mult_pad = 0` for `ℓ(λ) ≥ 11`, so a longer
  weight is useless to us.
- Reproduce their own `n = 3` example `(19, 7, 2^5)` at `δ = 12` from the same
  formula.  If it does not reproduce their stated example, the formula is wrong.
- **Resolve the factor-of-two ambiguity explicitly.**  The external audit flagged
  a possible factor of two in the degree.  If it resolves the other way the cell
  is `δ = 12`, `|λ| = 48` — a different and much cheaper experiment.  State which
  and why.

## 4. Task 3 — the experiment that does not need the multiplicity

**This is the point of the session.**  Expanding a degree-24 invariant in
16-variable coefficient space is hopeless.  But LMR's equations come from
degeneracy of the dual variety, and their concrete content is a
**divisibility**: for a form `P`, the construction produces a Hessian-derived
expression divisible by `P` exactly when the dual is degenerate to the stated
extent.

So evaluation reduces to a **remainder computation** — build the expression for
the given point, divide by `P`, report the remainder.  Zero remainder means the
equation vanishes there.  This is polynomial division in a small variable set,
not an expansion in coefficient space, and it is affordable.

Your first job is to state precisely, from the paper, what the expression is —
which bordered or extended Hessian, which minor size, which `k` — and to write
down the divisibility statement being tested.  Get that on paper before code.

### The four controls.  Run all four.

1. **`det_4` pencils.**  Remainder must be zero.  If not, the implementation is
   wrong and nothing downstream means anything.  Calibration, not a result.
2. **Generic quartics in the same variables.**  Remainder must be nonzero.  If
   generic quartics also give zero, the expression is identically zero.
3. **Reducible `ℓ·c` with `c` generic.**  The transfer control: `P_r ⊆ R_r`, so
   a nonzero remainder here is weaker than at a true padded permanent, and a
   zero here is more informative.
4. **The full ten-variable `ℓ·per_3`.**  Not a length-reduced restriction.  Not
   optional and not a formality: every evaluation control we have run to date was
   at a restriction to fewer variables, and restriction can manufacture
   accidental vanishing.  If controls 3 and 4 disagree, that disagreement is the
   session's most important output.

Report every remainder as an exact integer or rational object with the input
points recorded, and hand the set to the session-49 verifier.

## 5. Task 4 — the sharp question

If the remainder is nonzero at padded permanents, **that copy of the equation
separates** — a polynomial vanishing on `D_r` and not on `P_r`, at degree 24.

Then: at that weight, is `i_det > i_pad`?  Or are the two ideal subspaces equal
in dimension and different as subspaces?  The second is the case the external
critique has been pressing, and a concrete instance would be the most
informative thing the programme has produced — a demonstration, at a named cell,
that our statistic discards a separation that is actually there.  Even bounds on
`i_det` and `i_pad`, or a proof they are equal without computing either, is
worth more than further sweeping.

## 7. Success and failure

**Success:** weight independently derived, factor of two settled, four-control
evaluation run with exact remainders.

**Best outcome:** the equation separates while the multiplicities agree.

**A real failure worth reporting:** control 1 or 2 failing.  If the construction
does not vanish on determinant pencils, or vanishes on everything, stop and
report.  Do not adjust it until it passes and then present the adjusted version
as a result.
