## 1. The lemma *(proved)*

Let `lam = (lam_1 >= ... >= lam_r)` have blocks of equal parts `B_1, ..., B_t`
with sizes `k_b` and values `m_b`.  The Weyl-group stabiliser
`Stab_W(lam) = S_{k_1} x ... x S_{k_t}` acts on the `lam`-weight space of
`C[W]_delta = Sym^delta(Sym^n (C^r)^*)` by permuting variable indices within
blocks (permutation matrices `P_w`, `w in Stab`; on the coefficient
functionals `P_w c_alpha = c_{w alpha}` with no scalar, because `alpha!` is
`w`-invariant).

**Lemma.**  Every highest-weight vector `v` of weight `lam` satisfies

    P_w v = chi_lam(w) v,        chi_lam(w) = prod_b sgn(w|_{B_b})^{m_b}.

*Proof.*  `P_w v` has weight `w(lam) = lam` and lies in the irreducible
`G`-module `V_lam` generated by `v`, whose `lam`-weight space is
one-dimensional; so `P_w v = c(w) v`, and `c` is a character of `Stab_W(lam)`
intrinsic to `V_lam` (any two copies are `G`-isomorphic and `P_w in G`).  On
the leading-minor model of the highest-weight line,
`prod_k Delta_k^{lam_k − lam_{k+1}}`, a transposition `(i, i+1)` inside a block
of value `m` negates every `Delta_k` with `k >= i+1` and fixes the rest, for a
total of `(−1)^{lam_{i+1}} = (−1)^m`; transpositions generate.  ∎

*A second proof, worth recording because it is what the row-dedup code
silently checks.*  Restrict to the `sl_2` on indices `(i, i+1)` of a block of
value `m`.  The `lam`-weight space has `sl_2`-weight `0`, and an HWV is killed
by `E_{i,i+1}`, so it lies in the `sl_2`-trivial component (spin `j = 0`).
The permutation matrix `P_(i i+1)` is the `SL_2` Weyl element times
`diag(1, −1)`; the former acts on the zero-weight space of the spin-`j`
component by `(−1)^j`, the latter by `(−1)^{lam_{i+1}} = (−1)^m`.  On `j = 0`
that is `(−1)^m`.  (It also explains the free observation in the run logs
that `E_{i,i+1}` vanishes identically on `V_chi` whenever a block has value
`m = 1`: spins are bounded by `j <= m`, so the sign-isotypic part of a value-1
block is entirely spin 0.)

At rectangular `lam = (delta^r)` this is s33's `sign^delta` lemma
(`docs/e4_hunt.md` §4).

**Consequence.**  `HWV_lam(C[W]_delta) ⊆ V_chi`, the `chi_lam`-isotypic
component of the weight space, of dimension `n_chi ~ N_S / |Stab_W(lam)|`
(exactly: the number of `Stab`-orbits of weight-`lam` monomials whose
stabiliser lies in `ker chi_lam`).

## 2. The pipeline *(implemented; every rank in flint)*

- **Isotypic basis.**  Twisted orbit sums `b_O = sum_{m in O} chi(w_m) e_m`
  over `Stab`-orbits `O` of weight-`lam` monomials; an orbit whose stabiliser
  meets `chi^{-1}(−1)` has vanishing twisted sum and drops out (e.g. `(13,5,4,1,1)`:
  1824 monomials, 1166 orbits, 508 dropped, `n_chi = 658`).
- **Every simple raising operator**, restricted to `V_chi` (columns), rows
  indexed by target monomials of weight `lam' = lam + e_i − e_{i+1}`.  Rows
  in one orbit of the common stabiliser `H = Stab(lam) ∩ Stab(lam')` (the
  elements of `Stab(lam)` fixing `i` and `i+1`) are proportional by `chi(h)`:
  `(E b_O)_{h t} = chi(h) (E b_O)_t`.  One canonical representative per
  `H`-orbit is kept; a target fixed by some `h` with `chi(h) = −1` must have a
  zero row, and this is **asserted exactly** on every cell (a free correctness
  check inherited from s33's odd-`delta` swap-fixed rows).
- **Kernel.**  `n_chi <= 2500`: one flint `nullspace` on the full reduced
  matrix.  Above: the certified compressed route of `docs/e4_hunt.md` §4 —
  `Agg = P·M`, `P` random over `F_p` with `n_chi + 64` rows, assembled in
  256-row chunks as `(M^T @ P_chunk)^T` by scipy sparse int64 (the int64 bound
  `max|M| · (p−1) · max column fill < 2^62` is asserted from the actual matrix)
  and written straight into one flint `nmod_mat`; one flint `nullspace`.
  Certificate: `rank(Agg) <= rank_p(M) <= rank_Q(M) = n_chi − a(plethysm)`, and
  the assert `dim ker(Agg) = a` forces equality throughout, so `ker(Agg)` is
  the reduction of the rational HWV space.  Peak resident is governed by
  `8 n_chi^2` — three copies inside flint's `nullspace` (matrix, rref, the
  `n x n` nullspace buffer), `~2.4e-8 n_chi^2` GB at peak, consistent with
  s33's `2.5e-8`; frontier `n_chi ~ 15500` on this container (§6).
- **Evaluation** exactly as `wk8_s30_core.measure`: `a + 8` points per side,
  det points `det_4(sum s_i A_i)`, pad points the **true padded permanent**
  `x_0 · per_3(x_1..x_9)` under random linear substitutions
  (`per_padded(3,4)` through `restrict()`), contracted to `chi`-coordinates
  (`e[j] = sum_{m in O_j} chi(w_m) ev[m]`), then `mult = rank(E · K)`.
  Both house primes at every rank; `mult` must agree.

**Porting note (the thing that does *not* generalise).**  s33's one-operator
shortcut — `ker E_12` alone on `V_chi` — worked because at rectangular weight
the *even* conjugators `P_{(123)}`, `P_{(13)(24)}` carry `E_12` to `E_23`,
`E_34`, i.e. `A_4` is 2-transitive on the indices.  At a general weight
`Stab_W(lam)` is a proper Young subgroup, is not transitive on ordered pairs
(it does not even move indices between blocks), and `Ad(P_w) E_{i,i+1}` for
`w in Stab` is a non-simple root operator inside a block.  So **all `r − 1`
simple raising operators are applied**; only the isotypic restriction and the
`H`-orbit row dedup transfer.  (The s33 warning that the shortcut also fails
for three variables is the same fact from the other side.)

### 4.2 The criterion (★): when a highest-weight vector vanishes on the reducible locus — *proved*

Let `X_r = closure{l · c} ⊆ Sym^4 C^r` be the reducible-with-a-linear-factor
locus and `L_i = {F : x_i | F}`, the coordinate subspace `{c_alpha = 0 for
all alpha with alpha_i = 0}`, stabilised by the maximal parabolic `P_i`.
Bruhat gives `G = ∪_w B w P_r`, so `X_r = G · L_r = ∪_i B · L_i`.  A
highest-weight vector is a `B`-eigenvector, `v(b · y) = chi(b)^{-1} v(y)`, so
`v` vanishes on `B · L_i` iff on `L_i`; and `I(L_i)` is the monomial ideal
`(c_alpha : alpha_i = 0)`.  Hence, for a HWV `v` of weight `lam` and degree
`delta`,

    v ∈ I(X_r)   ⟺   every monomial of v has, for every i ∈ [r],
                     a factor c_alpha with alpha_i = 0.                 (★)

Two consequences fall out at once.

*(a) The automatic case.*  A monomial of weight `lam` and degree `delta` has
`delta` factors whose `i`-th exponents sum to `lam_i`; if `lam_i < delta`
some factor has `alpha_i = 0`.  So **if `lam_1 < delta`, every HWV of weight
`lam` lies in `I(X_r)`**, i.e. `mult_pad(lam, delta) = 0` for every `r` (as
`D_r^pad ⊆ X_r`) and **no cell with `lam_1 < delta` can ever be an
obstruction**.  This is the `n − m = 1` case of the Kadish–Landsberg padding
constraint (`S_lam` occurs in the coordinate ring of a padded orbit closure
`GL · l^{n−m} P` in degree `delta` only if `lam_1 >= (n − m) delta`; *Padded
polynomials, their cousins, and GCT*, 2014 — cited from memory, to be
checked against the paper), recovered here by a two-line argument.  It also
explains the classical-looking fact below: an `SL_r`-invariant of degree
`delta` of `r`-ary quartics has weight `(4 delta / r)^r`, and `4 delta / r <
delta` iff `r > 4` — every invariant of quinary or senary quartics vanishes
on the reducible locus (equivalently: `delta` copies of `l` distributed over
`4 delta / r < delta` epsilon tensors force two into one epsilon).

*(b) The exact case.*  Only the indices with `lam_i >= delta` can fail (★);
with `M_red` the (`Stab`-invariant) set of weight-`lam` monomials satisfying
(★), the **point-free reducibility multiplicity**

    mult_red(lam, delta) := a − dim( HWV_lam ∩ span M_red )

is computable from the HWV kernel alone, no evaluation points, no primes
beyond the linear algebra.  `mult_pad = mult_red` **exactly at `r = 5`**
(`D_5^pad = X_5`: `per_3`-pencils are dense in quinary cubics, `dim 39 = 5 +
35 − 1`, s30's table), and at `r = 6` `mult_pad <= mult_red` with a strict gap
**iff a permanent-specific equation exists** — the prereg's sharper falsifier
in exact form.  `wk9_s36_red.py` computes it from the banked kernels;
`results/s36_red_table.md` lists it against the point-based `mult_pad` at
every banked cell — **they agree at every one of them**, at every bite and at
every `ell = 6` cell.  *(Post-hoc: (★) and `mult_red` were derived after the
first bite, and are labelled so; nothing pre-registered was altered.)*

For the `(8,4,4,4,4)` vector, (★) holds outright — not one of its 19834
monomials survives restriction to any `L_i` — so `mult_pad <= 1` is
**proved**, the pad evaluations certify `>= 1`, and `mult_det = 2` is a
rank-attaining certificate: `D((8,4,4,4,4), 6) = −1` exactly.
