# Where the ideals become visible — and a convention error in the machinery

Session 29, branch `s29-visible`, 2026-09-01.  Clone tip `1203fe4`
(`1203fe4` verified as an ancestor; sessions 27 and 28 have **not** landed, so
the rank machinery is rebuilt here from scratch).
Pre-registration: `results/PREREG_s29.md`, committed before any computation.

**Headline.**  The programme has never measured a cell with `mult < a`.  That
is not a neutral fact: **a measurement that returns the maximum cannot detect a
wrong kernel basis**, because a wrong basis of a right-dimensional space still
generically has full rank.  So the isotypic rank algorithm's central
convention had never been tested.  It is wrong.  The rule quoted in
`docs/isotypic_rank.md` §1 and used by session 26,

    E_ij . c_alpha = alpha_j c_{alpha - eps_j + eps_i},

is the action on the **monomials** `e^alpha` of `Sym^n V`, not on the
**coefficient functionals** `c_alpha = e^alpha / alpha!`.  The correct rule is

    E_ij . c_alpha = (alpha_i + 1) c_{alpha - eps_j + eps_i}.

The two differ by the diagonal rescaling `c_alpha -> alpha! c_alpha`, so they
give the **same** kernel dimension `a` and **different** kernel vectors — which
is exactly why every `a` calibration passed and no `mult` calibration could
fail.  Corrected, the algorithm reproduces 48 World A cells, **41 of them with
`mult < a`**, and — reassuringly — every previously reported number is
unchanged.

---

## 1. The error, and the test that finds it

`W = Sym^n V^*`; `C[W]_delta` is generated by the coefficient functionals
`c_alpha(F) = ` coefficient of `y^alpha` in `F`.  Under the pairing
`<e^beta, y^alpha> = delta_{alpha,beta} alpha!`, `c_alpha = e^alpha / alpha!`.
The Lie algebra acts on `Sym^n V` by the derivation
`E_ij e^alpha = alpha_j e^{alpha - eps_j + eps_i}`, so on the functionals

    E_ij c_alpha = (alpha'! / alpha!) alpha_j c_{alpha'} = (alpha_i + 1) c_{alpha'},
    alpha' = alpha - eps_j + eps_i.

**The witness.**  Take `n = 4`, `r = 2`, `lam = (4,4)`, `delta = 2`, and the
locus `{l^3 m}` — which is `D_r` for `x_0^3 x_1`, i.e. the `m = 1` padded
permanent, and is the tangent developable of session 24's World A.  Session 24
derived, by a completely different route (substitution ranks and Gaussian
binomials, no highest-weight vectors anywhere), `mult_tau((4d-b,b),d) =
[b <= d and b != 1]`.  At `d = 2`, `b = 4` that is **0**, against `a = 1`.

    old rule:  mult = 1     (wrong)
    new rule:  mult = 0     (correct)

By hand, the weight-`(4,4)` highest-weight vector is the classical quadratic
invariant `I = a_0a_4 - 4a_1a_3 + 3a_2^2`, which in plain coefficients is
`12 c_0c_4 - 3 c_1c_3 + c_2^2`.  Under the old rule `E c_i = i c_{i-1}` this
gives `E I = 45 c_0c_3 - 5 c_1c_2 != 0`; under the new rule
`E c_i = (5-i) c_{i-1}` it gives `E I = 0`.  And `I` does vanish on
`(x+y)^3 x`: coefficients `(1,3,3,1,0)`, so `a = (1, 3/4, 1/2, 1/4, 0)` and
`I = 0 - 4(3/4)(1/4) + 3(1/2)^2 = 0`.  The new rule is right.

**The discriminating battery** (`analysis/wk8_s29_discrim.py`): all
`lam = (4d-b, b)` with `a > 0`, `d <= 7` — 48 cells, **41 discriminating** —
against session 24's closed forms for `{l^3 m}` and `{l^4}`.  All 48 pass.

## 3. A: the first strict inequality — where it is, and where it is not

`D_3^{det_4}` is **all** ternary quartics (Jacobian rank 15 of 15), so
`mult_det = a` at every length-3 weight and any length-3 weight with
`mult_pad < a` is an immediate strict `D < 0`.  Swept:

* **length exactly 3, `delta <= 7`, all 135 weights with `a >= 1`** (complete,
  no cap binding): `mult_det = mult_{per_3^pad} = a` at every one.
* **length `<= 3`, `delta <= 5`, 64 weights**: same.

So for the real padded permanent `per_3^pad` there is **no strict `D < 0`
anywhere in the swept range**.  The reducible-quartic locus `{l . c}` has
codimension 3 at `r = 3` and codimension 12 at `r = 4`, and its ideal is
invisible in every isotypic slice reached.

**But the machinery can now be shown to see ideals**, which before this session
it could not.  Descending the padding chain at `n = 4`,

    m=1: {l^3 m}   m=2: {l^2 q}   m=3: {l c}   det: everything (at r <= 3)
    D^{per_1} subset D^{per_2} subset D^{per_3} subset D^{det_4}

the two lower members are visible at once.  The first cells in the programme's
history with `mult < a`:

| `delta` | `lam` | `a` | `mult_det` | `mult_{m=3}` | `mult_{m=2}` | `mult_{m=1}` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | (4,4)     | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | **0** |
| 3 | (4,4,4)   | 1 | 1 | 1 | **0** | **0** |
| 4 | (8,6,2)   | 2 | 2 | 2 | **1** | **0** |
| 5 | (12,6,2)  | 4 | 4 | 4 | **3** | **0** |
| 5 | (10,6,4)  | 4 | 4 | 4 | **1** | **0** |

The `m = 3` column — the padded permanent the programme actually cares about —
is `a` throughout.  The ideals become visible exactly one step further down the
chain than the object of interest.

**What a strict `D < 0` would have been worth** (registered in advance, and
repeated here because it is easy to overstate): nothing for separation.  `D < 0`
says the padded permanent's closure has *fewer* functions than the
determinant's, which is what the session-27 containment theorem forces and is
the wrong direction for GCT.  Its only value is calibration — and that value
has now been obtained from the `m = 1, 2` members instead.

## 4. C: the subspace protocol, run non-vacuously 18 times

Containment of varieties reverses on ideals, so the chain predicts

    U_det  subset  U_{m=3}  subset  U_{m=2}  subset  U_{m=1}

at every weight, where `U` is the ideal's slice inside `C^a`, computed as the
kernel of the evaluation matrix restricted to a fixed basis of `ker R` (two
primes, dimensions cross-checked against `a - mult`).

At length `<= 3`, `delta <= 5`: **18 non-vacuous tests** (both subspaces
nonzero), **all pass**.  Sample:

    delta=4  lam=(8,6,2)   a=2  U_{m=2} dim 1  subset  U_{m=1} dim 2   OK
    delta=5  lam=(12,6,2)  a=4  U_{m=2} dim 1  subset  U_{m=1} dim 4   OK
    delta=5  lam=(10,6,4)  a=4  U_{m=2} dim 3  subset  U_{m=1} dim 4   OK
    delta=5  lam=(8,6,6)   a=2  U_{m=2} dim 2  subset  U_{m=1} dim 2   OK

The `det ⊂ m3` and `m3 ⊂ m2` tests are vacuous throughout (`U_det = U_{m3} = 0`
at every length-`<= 3` weight), for the structural reason in §3.  The tool is
built, validated, and ready for the length-5 version; what it needs is a weight
where the two ideals of interest are both nonzero, and §3 says that is not at
length `<= 3`.

## 5. B: pinning `e` — attempted, not completed

`e >= 6` stands (no `SL_4`-invariant of quaternary quartics in degrees 1, 2, 3,
5; and `mult_det((4^4),4) = a = 1`, so `e != 4`).  The deciding bit is
`mult_det((6,6,6,6), 6)`, whose weight space is **12,652**.  All the `delta = 7`
alternatives are worse — `(10,6,6,6)`: 39,761, `(9,7,6,6)`: 47,204,
`(8,8,6,6)`: 50,059, `(7,7,7,7)`: 57,232 — so `(6^4)` is the cheapest
discriminating cell and there is no cheaper flank.

Two attempts, both abandoned honestly:

1. A **blocked** elimination mod `p = 46337` in float64 (so the rank-`k`
   updates run through BLAS).  It was **wrong**: the panel's pivot rows were
   not themselves reduced before the trailing rank-`k` update, so the
   multipliers did not form a unit-triangular factor.  It reported rank 11,588
   where `a = 1` demands 12,651 — caught by the built-in `a` self-check, which
   is the reason that self-check is there.
2. A corrected **unblocked** version, vectorised per pivot, running at roughly
   2,000 pivots in 45 s early on.  It was killed when the convention error of
   §1 was found, since its kernel vector would have been the wrong one.

`e` is therefore **untested**, not confirmed and not refuted.  My
pre-registered prediction was `e >= 7`, against the integrator's `e = 6`; it
stands unresolved.  The cost is now known precisely: one 12,652-column exact
elimination with a correct unblocked implementation, roughly 20–40 minutes of
numpy per prime, and it should be the first thing the next session runs.
