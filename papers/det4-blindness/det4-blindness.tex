\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Scope.} This is a negative-results paper with an exact scope. It concerns one comparison:
the variety $\Dff\subset\Sym^4\bC^5$ of quinary quartics with a $4\times4$ linear determinantal
representation (dimension $50$, affine) against the padding locus
$\Pf=\overline{\{\ell\cdot C\}}$ (dimension $39$, affine), which is what the padded
$3\times3$ permanent becomes in five-row cells. It produces \emph{no} equation, \emph{no}
separation, \emph{no} multiplicity gap, and \emph{no} theorem that a separating equation of length
five does not exist: one exists in some five-row cell of degree at most $4^{49}$,
\emph{conditional on refined B\'ezout}, and none has been written down. There is no five-row
determinant equation \emph{known} to be nonzero on padding.

What the paper does is explain why the natural instruments are blind, and it keeps the
explanation's scope exact. (i) A closed condition extracted from a semicontinuous integer
statistic of a quartic can separate $\Dff$ from a padding point only in one direction for each
type of semicontinuity (proved from the definitions). (ii) In five variables, instrument by
instrument, the direction is the wrong one. Every rank-threshold ideal of the first Koszul
differential of the Jacobian lies in $I(\Dff)\cap I(\Pf)$. The rank-threshold ideals of the
higher differentials on $\Dff$ are zero, conditional on $\operatorname{grade}=\operatorname{height}$
in a polynomial ring. Every $\SL_5$-invariant of positive degree vanishes on $\Pf$. Every
polynomial in the minors through which kernel data are extracted vanishes on padding. Every
polynomial in the hyperplane-section discriminant vanishes on padding. (iii) Two local
statistics bite on linear sections of $\det_4$: the rank-$6$ bound on the second fundamental
form, which is the right-way statistic of Landsberg--Manivel--Ressayre, and the rank-$36$ bound on the middle
catalecticant, which is itself reversed. Both are vacuous exactly when the number of variables
is $N\le 8$. At $N=6,7,8$ the rank-threshold kills are \emph{assessed, not proved}. (iv)
Restricted to padding, the separation problem runs the right way on the cubic factor,
conditional on the cap theorem (ADOPTED). What is missing is a lift to quartics, and
$\Dff\cap\Pf$ is strictly larger than $\{\ell\cdot C: C\in D_{35}\}$. We include the evidence
that the instruments work where there is something to see: the unpadded $n=3$ control, with
$D=+1$ at every rung $\delta\ge12$. We also include one worked example of the method: the
$(4^5)$ cell in degree $5$, where $D=-1$. Every claim carries its record label, source packet,
commit and review lineage at the point of use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The comparison}
The padded permanent in this programme is $z\per_3$, compared with $\det_4$ in sixteen
variables. A \emph{five-row cell} is a pair $(d,\lambda)$ with
$\lambda\vdash 4d$ of length $5$. For such cells the comparison reduces to five variables
(\S\ref{sec:setting}): on one side the closure $\Dff$ of the quinary determinantal quartics, on
the other the closure $\Pf$ of the products $\ell\cdot C$ of a linear form and a cubic. The
programme wants a \emph{multiplicity obstruction}: a cell where padding has strictly larger
multiplicity than the determinant.

\subsection{What this paper does not claim}
Four achievements are kept apart throughout. They are never conflated, and this paper achieves
none of them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \emph{necessary source condition} (a condition on the source space of a boundary
      instrument; \S\ref{sec:example});
\item a \emph{coefficient equation} (a polynomial in the $70$ coefficients of a quinary quartic,
      proved to vanish on $\Dff$);
\item a \emph{separation on padding} (a coefficient equation with an exhibited actual padding
      point where it is nonzero);
\item a \emph{positive multiplicity gap} ($D>0$ in some cell).
\end{enumerate}
\prov{standing rule}{B20-12 ledger \S1 @ \cm{da803892}; B22-02 \S0 @ \cm{e22a41b1}; B22-01 \S0 @ \cm{53bdb31e}}{integrator; restated by every Batch 20--22 packet}

Separation exists and is not the issue. The issue is that nobody has one. The accurate
phrase is: \emph{no five-row determinant equation known to be nonzero on padding}. It is
never ``none known''. Five-row $\det_4$ equations of degree $300$ are on the record
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cap}), and they vanish on padding (Theorem~\ref{thm:gkzB}). A separating
equation exists, conditionally, in degree at most $4^{49}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:E}), and this paper
does not contradict that.

Nor does this paper prove that no construction of an equation of length $5$ to $8$ nonzero on
padding exists. Its kills are only as strong as their labels. Some are PROVED statements that a
named polynomial or ideal vanishes on padding. Others are ASSESSED statements that no recipe
exists on the record. An ASSESSED-kill is not an impossibility.

\subsection{The thesis, and its exact scope}
The following statement is \emph{not} a theorem of the record. It has no single record label.
It is an assembly of the labelled results cited in each clause, and it is no stronger than the
weakest of them. The gaps list records the composite's status (GAPS~G-16).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Thesis.} \emph{The blindness is structural at five variables, and it can be
characterised.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A closed condition from a semicontinuous integer statistic separates $\Dff$ from a
padding point only if the statistic is lower semicontinuous and larger on padding, or upper
semicontinuous and smaller on padding (Lemma~\ref{lem:sc}, \cid{C13}).
\item[(b)] In five variables, each named instrument built from the singularities of $F$ fails
that test on padding. This covers the rank-threshold ideals of every Koszul differential
(Theorems~\ref{thm:gkzB} and~\ref{thm:koszul}; the latter CONDITIONAL on (T2)), kernel data
extracted by minors (Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel}), all $\SL_5$-invariants and every
$\GL_5$-covariant into cubics (Lemma~\ref{lem:nullcone}), and the section discriminant
(Fact~\ref{fact:disc}). The step ``padding is more singular, hence fails the test'' is proved
instrument by instrument at $N=5$ and at the stated $N=16$ gradings. At $N=6,7,8$ it is a
heuristic (Ruling~\ref{rul:scope}).
\item[(c)] $N=5,\dots,8$ is exactly the range in which the two classical local statistics that
bite on linear sections of $\det_4$ are both vacuous (Lemma~\ref{lem:window}). Only one of
them, the second-fundamental-form rank, runs the right way. The other, the catalecticant rank,
is itself reversed.
\item[(d)] The reversal belongs to the quartic-side \emph{mechanisms}, not to the separation
problem restricted to padding. On the cubic factor the problem runs the right way
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic}), conditional on the cap theorem. What is missing is a lift, and the
covariant lifts are dead.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

The instruments are not broken: the positive control of \S\ref{sec:control} shows them producing
$D=+1$, a theorem at every rung, where the comparison has content. The blindness is a property
of padding at five rows.

\subsection{Multiplicities and the obstruction}
For a cell $(d,\lambda)$ let $a$ be the multiplicity of $S_\lambda\bC^5$ in
$\Sym^d(\Sym^4\bC^5)$, and let $m_{\det},m_{\mathrm{pad}}$ be its multiplicities in the degree-$d$
coordinate rings of $\Dff$ and $\Pf$. Write $i_X=a-m_X$ for the multiplicity in the ideal, and
$D=m_{\mathrm{pad}}-m_{\det}=i_{\det}-i_{\mathrm{pad}}$.

\begin{lemma}[\cid{C04}; deficit decomposition]\label{lem:deficit}
Let $v_A,v_B$ be vectors in a rational $G$-module with orbit closures $A,B$. Let $m_A,m_B$ be the
Peter--Weyl counts $\dim(S_\lambda^*)^{H}$ for their stabilisers, and let $\mathrm{def}_A,\mathrm{def}_B$ be the
boundary deficits $m_X(\lambda)-\mult_\lambda\bC[X]\ge0$. Then
\[
\mult_\lambda\bC[B]-\mult_\lambda\bC[A]=\bigl[m_B(\lambda)-m_A(\lambda)\bigr]-\bigl[\mathrm{def}_B(\lambda)-\mathrm{def}_A(\lambda)\bigr].
\]
Moreover, if $B\subseteq A$ are $G$-stable closed cones, then $\bC[A]\twoheadrightarrow\bC[B]$, so
$\mult_\lambda\bC[A]\ge\mult_\lambda\bC[B]$ for every $\lambda$.
\end{lemma}
\prov{PROVED (elementary; Peter--Weyl, injectivity of restriction and surjectivity under containment, each standard)}{\texttt{docs/obstruction\_power.md} \S1, Lemma~1 and (SUR) @ \cm{82633a60}}{producer only (session 24); no reviewer row located (GAPS G-14)}

This is the frame the paper works in. A positive $D$ refutes $\Pf\subseteq\Dff$ by the containment
clause. The Peter--Weyl part depends only on the two stabilisers. The deficit part depends only
on the two boundaries. Every instrument in \S\ref{sec:reversal} is an attempt to see a boundary
difference.

\begin{ruling}[\cid{C05}; when one equation is a gap]\label{rul:G5}
Suppose one has a certified upper bound $B\ge m_{\det}$ and a certified padding floor $r\le m_{\mathrm{pad}}$
from evaluations at exhibited actual padding points. If $r>B$, then $D>0$. When $a=1$, a
highest-weight vector \emph{proved} to vanish on $\Dff$ and nonzero at one actual padding point
gives $D=1$: in that case separation \emph{is} a gap. When $a\ge2$, one equation nonzero on
padding gives only $i_{\det}\ge1$ and $m_{\mathrm{pad}}\ge1$, never $D>0$. One nonzero
determinant evaluation closes a cell ($D\le0$) only when $a=1$.
\end{ruling}
\prov{ruling (gate G5$'$, ACCEPTED)}{B20-10 \S8, R22 @ \cm{6915ae6f}}{reviewer B20-10}

\section{The direction reversal, instrument by instrument}\label{sec:reversal}

\subsection{The semicontinuity form}
\begin{lemma}[\cid{C13}; semicontinuity form of the reversal]\label{lem:sc}
Let $q$ be an integer-valued function of the quartic $F$. If $q$ is upper semicontinuous, the
closed conditions it defines are $\{q\ge c\}$. They separate $\Dff$ from a padding point $P$
iff $q(P)<c\le q(\text{generic }\Dff)$. If $q$ is lower semicontinuous, they are $\{q\le c\}$,
and they separate iff $q(\text{generic }\Dff)\le c<q(P)$. So separation by a semicontinuous
statistic needs an l.s.c.\ quantity that is \emph{larger} on padding, or a u.s.c.\ quantity that
is \emph{smaller} on padding.
\end{lemma}
\prov{PROVED (from the definitions)}{B22-02 \S1.1, ledger L1 @ \cm{e22a41b1}}{producer only; B22-10 did not rule on \S1.1 separately, and it rules the application ``padding is more singular, hence a larger Milnor algebra'' at $N=6,7,8$ to be plausible and unproved (S19)}

Padding is more singular than the determinant: its singular locus has codimension $2$ against
$4$ {\footnotesize\textsf{[B20-02 \S2 @ \cm{7de65d7c}; producer only]}}. A singularity-driven
statistic therefore tends to fail the applicable test. That sentence is a heuristic, not a
theorem (B22-10 \S6 row~1). The rest of this section shows, for each named instrument, where this is proved
and where it is not.

\begin{lemma}[\cid{C14}; a separating polynomial is generically nonzero]\label{lem:generic}
If $f(P)\ne0$ at one point $P$, then $f$ is nonzero on a dense open subset of $\Sym^4\bC^5$, in
particular at quartics with finite singular locus. No polynomial can be ``supported on'' the
positive-dimensional part of padding's singular locus.
\end{lemma}
\prov{PROVED}{B22-02 Lemma~1.2 (L2) @ \cm{e22a41b1}}{producer B22-02; reviewer B22-10 S17 (READ)}

\subsection{Kernel data}
\begin{lemma}[\cid{C15}; kernel covariants inherit the reversal]\label{lem:kernel}
Let $M(F)$ be a matrix with polynomial entries in $F$, let $r=\max_{F\in\Dff}\rank M(F)$, and
suppose $\rank M(P)<r$. Then every polynomial without constant term in the $r\times r$ minors of
$M(F)$ vanishes at $P$. This covers the Pl\"ucker coordinates of $\ker M(F)$ and $\operatorname{im}M(F)$
at generic points of $\Dff$, and every covariant built polynomially from them.
Instance: for $M=M_7=d_1^{(7)}$, padding has rank $244$ at the two $\Pf$ points of B20-02 and the
P2 pencil has rank $299$. The determinant's one extra syzygy class at $k=7$ is extracted by
$299$-minors, so every construction that uses it vanishes on padding. The same holds for the ideal
of the twenty nodes for $k\ge8$.
\end{lemma}
\prov{PROVED, with a scope caveat: it kills constructions that extract the kernel through $r\times r$ minors, not a kernel covariant obtained otherwise (e.g.\ from the pencil, on the source side). The instance's ranks are CERTIFIED exact over $\bQ$}{B22-02 Lemma~1.3 (L3) @ \cm{e22a41b1}; ranks B20-02 \S7.3 @ \cm{7de65d7c}}{producer B22-02; reviewer B22-10 S17 (READ; caveat pre-formed)}

\subsection{Invariant theory: the null cone}
\begin{lemma}[\cid{C16}; padding is in the $\SL_5$ null cone]\label{lem:nullcone}
Let $\lambda(s)=\operatorname{diag}(s^{-4},s,s,s,s)\in\SL_5$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\lambda(s)\cdot x_1C\to0$ as $s\to\infty$, so $\ell\cdot C$ is $\SL_5$-unstable and every
$\SL_5$-invariant of positive degree vanishes on $\Pf$. In particular this holds for every
equation of $\Dff$ in a rectangular cell $((4d/5)^5)$.
\item[(b)] If $\Psi:\Sym^4\bC^5\to\Sym^t\bC^5$ is $\SL_5$-equivariant and homogeneous of degree
$e$, then $\Psi(\ell\cdot C)$ is divisible by $\ell^{\lceil (t+e)/5\rceil}$.
\item[(c)] A $\GL_5$-covariant $\Psi:\Sym^4\to\Sym^3$ of degree $e$ has $e\ge7$, and
$\Psi(\ell\cdot C)=\ell^2 m$ with $m$ linear or $0$. That is $\det\operatorname{diag}(\ell,\ell,m)$, so it
lies in $D_{35}$.

\section{Why the window is five to eight variables}\label{sec:window}

\begin{lemma}[\cid{C31}; the window is exactly $N\le8$]\label{lem:window}
Let $F=\det_4\circ\Lambda$ with $\Lambda:\bC^N\to\mathrm{Mat}_4$ linear.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] At a point $x$ of $X_F$ with $\rank\Lambda(x)=3$, the second fundamental form of $X_F$ is
the pull-back of that of $X_{\det}$, which has rank $6$ (at $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,0)$ it is
$-\sum_{i\le3}b_{i4}b_{4i}$ on $b_{44}=0$). So $\rank II_F\le6$ on a space of dimension $N-2$.
\item[(ii)] The middle catalecticant satisfies $\rank\Cat_{2,2}(F)\le36$, the number of $2\times2$
minors of a $4\times4$ matrix, on a source of dimension $N(N+1)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Both bounds are vacuous exactly for $N\le8$ ($N-2\le6$ and $N(N+1)/2\le36$), and both bite
from $N=9$. Bound (i) is the right-way statistic: padding's second fundamental form is
nondegenerate. Bound (ii) is reversed: for $z\per_3$ the span of second partials is smaller
than $36$.
\end{lemma}
