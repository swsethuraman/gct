## 0. A structural prediction, registered before measuring

I think the nineteen cells are decided by a containment, not by a computation,
and I am registering the argument now so that the measurement is a test of it.

**Claim.**  `D_4^{per_3^pad} ⊆ D_4^{det_4}`, hence
`mult_lam C[closure(per_3^pad)]_delta <= mult_lam C[closure(det_4)]_delta`
for **every** `lam` of length `<= 4` and **every** `delta`.

*Argument.*  Restricting `x_0 . per_3` to a 4-plane gives `ell(s) . c(s)` with
`ell` the restriction of `x_0` and `c` the restriction of `per_3`; since
`per_3` is dense in the 4-ary cubics (session 26: rank 20 of 20 at `r = 4`),
`D_4^pad` is the whole reducible locus `closure{ell . c}`, of dimension
`4 + C(6,3) - 1 = 23` — which is the measured rank.  Now every 4-ary cubic is
`3x3` linear-determinantal (classical for smooth cubic surfaces; density is
session 26's `n = 3, r = 4` row, rank 20 of 20), so for generic `c` write
`c = det_3 M(s)` and then

    ell . c  =  det_4 diag( ell(s), M(s) ),

a `4x4` linear determinantal representation.  `D_4^{det_4}` is closed, so it
contains the closure of the generic reducible quartics, i.e. all of `D_4^pad`. ∎

Containment of varieties gives a surjection of coordinate rings, hence the
multiplicity inequality at every length-4 weight.  So:

**P1.  `D = mult_per^pad − mult_det <= 0` at all nineteen cells, and at every
weight of length `<= 4` at every degree.**  I agree with the integrator's
prior, but for a structural reason rather than the dimension heuristic — and
note that the dimension heuristic on its own is *not* a valid argument, since
isotypic multiplicities are not determined by dimensions.
*Falsifier:* any weight with `mult_per^pad > mult_det`.  If that appears, my
claim is wrong and I stop and report per kill criterion 1.

**Where the argument stops, and where the programme should go.**  At `r = 5`
the same construction needs every 5-ary cubic to be `3x3`-determinantal, and it
is not (session 26: rank 29 of 35).  So the containment argument breaks exactly
at length 5, and I predict the genuinely open cells are at
`ell(lam) >= 5`, not at `ell(lam) = 4`.  I will compute where that gate opens.

## 1. The other pre-registered numbers

**P2a.  `mult_det = a` at all nineteen** (i.e. no weight with `mult_det < a`).
Reasoning: `D_4^{det_4}` has codimension 1, so its ideal is principal,
generated by an irreducible `SL_4`-invariant `F` of quaternary quartics of some
degree `e` (a `GL_4`-semi-invariant of weight `det^w` on `Sym^4 C^4` has
`w = e`).  Then `mult_det(lam,delta) = a(lam,delta) − a(lam − e.1, delta − e)`,
which equals `a` whenever `delta < e`.  I predict `e > 5`, so all nineteen give
`mult_det = a`.  Confidence: moderate — I do not know `e` and am not going to
pretend I recall it.  *Falsifier:* any nineteen-cell with `mult_det < a`; that
would pin `e <= 5` and I would then measure `e` directly.

**P2b.  `mult_per^pad < a` at all nineteen.**  `D_4^pad` has codimension 12 in
`Sym^4 C^4`, so its ideal is large and very unlikely to miss every one of
nineteen isotypic components at `delta = 5`.  I predict **19 of 19** strictly
below `a`.  *Falsifier:* any cell with `mult_per^pad = a`.

**P2c.** Combining P2a and P2b, I predict `D < 0` **strictly** at all nineteen,
not merely `<= 0`.

**P3.  My independent rank implementation agrees with session 26 on all five
cells and on the 20 calibration weights.**  A disagreement would mean one of

question as well.  I would stop and report rather than adjudicate.

**P4.  The gate table of §1 of the brief reproduces exactly**, including

of that table differing.

## 2. What I expect the verdict to be

That `delta = 5` closes — not because the determinant happens to win a race,
but because at length 4 the padded permanent's stratum is *contained* in the
determinant's, so no length-4 weight can ever carry an obstruction at any
degree.  That would mean the brief's gate is measuring the wrong axis: the
right gate is `ell(lam) >= 5`, where the containment provably fails, and the
next session should be given the `ell >= 5` gate rather than `delta = 6`.
I expect to have to say that the nineteen cells were not, in the end, the first
genuinely open cells — the first genuinely open cells are one length further
up.
