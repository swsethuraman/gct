## 0. Two structural observations, registered before measuring

**(i) The cheapest strict inequality is at length 3, not length 4.**  The brief
directs the search to `delta = 5, 6, 7` at length `<= 4`.  But the same
reduction that makes length 4 tractable makes length 3 cheaper still, and
length 3 is *already* a place where the two sides provably differ:

    D_3^{det_4} = ALL ternary quartics          (Jacobian rank 15 of 15)
    D_3^{pad}   = the REDUCIBLE ternary quartics {ell.c}, dim 12 of 15, codim 3.

So `mult_det = a` at **every** length-3 weight, and any length-3 weight with
`mult_pad < a` is immediately a strict `D < 0`.  A codimension-3 subvariety
should show its ideal at small degree.  I will sweep length 3 from `delta = 2`
upward before touching length 4.

**(ii) The non-vacuous subspace test the brief asks for is almost certainly out
of reach at length 4, for a reason worth stating in advance.**  Part C needs a
cell with `mult_det < a` AND `mult_pad < a`.  At length `<= 3` the determinant
is dense, so `U_det = 0` and the test is vacuous.  At length 4 the determinant's
ideal is principal, generated by `F` of degree `e >= 6`, so `U_det != 0` only at
weights `lam` that are `(e,e,e,e)` plus a degree-4 exponent vector — every part
at least 6, hence `|lam| >= 24` and weight spaces in the tens of thousands.
I predict every such cell is beyond this session's compute.

**So I will build and validate the subspace tool on a pair that is both
non-vacuous and programme-relevant**: the padded permanents for `m = 2` and
`m = 3` against each other.  `per_2^pad = x_0^2 . per_2` restricts to `ell^2 q`,
and `ell^2 q = ell . (ell q)` is reducible, so

    D_r^{per_2^pad}  subset  D_r^{per_3^pad}  subset  D_r^{det_4},

a genuine chain of three programme objects.  At length 3 the first two ideals
are both nonzero and cheap, so `U_{per_3^pad} ⊆ U_{per_2^pad}` is a real
containment test with both subspaces nonzero.  That is the dress rehearsal the
brief wants; I will say plainly that it is a substitute for the det/pad version
and why.
