## Exact maps and the single decision

Order the 65 independent retained coefficients by descending lexicographic order of their degree-four exponents, omitting the five squarefree monomials. Let n(s) be the resulting coefficients of the explicit fifty-parameter normal form N in STRUCTURAL_MAP.md. Each n_alpha is an integer polynomial of degree at most five in s. Let p(T) be the coefficients of (z per3) composed with the general 10-by-5 matrix T, in row order z,y11,y12,y13,y21,y22,y23,y31,y32,y33. These are integer polynomials of degree four in fifty entries.

Let G=(y_alpha-n_alpha(s)) in Q[s_1,...,s_50,y_1,...,y_65]. Elimination gives

    J=G intersect Q[y]=ker(h -> h(n(s)))=I(Y_5).

The first equality follows because substituting y=n(s) identifies the graph quotient with Q[s]; no radical or sampled equations are substituted for this kernel. Its zero set is the closed image Y_5. The exact remaining question is J subseteq ker(h -> h(p(T))). This is equivalent to the original projected actual-padding inclusion, including every closure boundary.

**One sufficient finite artifact:** an exact certificate for this ideal inclusion, or one element of J with nonzero actual-padding restriction. A verified elimination basis with graph-membership witnesses and Buchberger reductions is one format; a sparse h and a verified exact T is the preferable positive format. The following explicit degree cap makes a single finite linear-algebra alternative precise, even though it is far too large to execute.

## Optional universal cap, not a feasible research degree

**C_DUBE — ADOPTED, PRIMARY statement level; proof not independently audited.** Dubé, *The Structure of Polynomial Ideals and Gröbner Bases*, SIAM J. Comput. 19(4), 1990, Corollary 8.3, p.773, bounds a Gröbner basis for an ideal generated in degree at most d in n variables, for any admissible order, by 2(d^2/2+d)^(2^(n-1)). The primary article was read at the abstract and section 8, especially the affine corollary. [Primary article](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~r5olivei/courses/2025-spring-cs860/D90.pdf), [publisher record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0219053).

For this graph, n=115 and d<=5. A convenient integer overbound is

    Delta = 2 * 18^(2^114).

**PROVED modulo C_DUBE:** J is generated by polynomials of degree at most Delta. Use an order eliminating all s variables; its elimination-basis subset generates J and inherits the degree bound. Therefore inclusion is decided by the one finite coefficient space H=Q[y]_(degree<=Delta). This is not an assertion that any useful equation has such an enormous minimal degree.

For any chosen cutoff d, including Delta, specify exact monomial bases and matrices

    A_d = binom(65+d,65),
    B_D(d) = binom(50+5d,50),
    B_P(d) = binom(50+4d,50),
    Q_D : H_d -> Q[s]_(degree<=5d),
    Q_P : H_d -> Q[T]_(degree<=4d).

Then

    rank([Q_D;Q_P]) > rank Q_D

is equivalent to a separator in H_d. At d=Delta, equality certifies whole-ring containment, modulo C_DUBE; strict inequality certifies separation. For a smaller d, equality closes only that cutoff. No rank here has been computed.

If a rational kernel vector v gives h with h(p(T)) nonzero, that polynomial has total degree at most 4d. Induction on variables shows a nonzero polynomial of individual degrees at most 4d cannot vanish on the full integer grid {0,...,4d}^50. Thus an exact integer T exists on a finite specified grid. This proves finite reconstructibility, not authorization for a grid hunt. An explicit h and one exact nonzero T, with their identity checks, would be the delivered positive certificate; abstract rank inequality alone would be labelled existential.

## Sparsity, bit growth, memory, and stop rule

The input graph has 65 equations in 115 variables, degree at most five, with at most 576 raw signed determinant terms per n_alpha plus its y term. Each padding coordinate has at most 144 raw terms (six permanent terms, at most 24 assignments of four variable choices for a fixed quartic exponent). These are conservative raw bounds; no supports were enumerated.

A column at coefficient degree at most d has at most 576^d raw determinant products and at most 144^d padding products. Each collected integer matrix entry has absolute value at most 576^d, hence at most b=2+ceil(d log2 576) bits including sign. A fully dense combined matrix stores A_d*(B_D(d)+B_P(d)) entries, before pivots and copies. Exponent vectors also need 50*ceil(log2(5d+1)) bits per source monomial, excluding indexing overhead. Fraction-free elimination can be priced through minors: an s-by-s determinant of entries bounded by 2^b has bit size at most 1+s*b+(s/2)log2(s), with s<=A_d. Dense arithmetic work is on the order of (B_D+B_P)*A_d^2 operations, apart from expansion. These are symbolic price bounds, not promised runtime.

Even at d=1 the deliberately unstructured determinant matrix has 66*binom(55,5)=229,598,226 positions: four bytes each would be 918,392,904 bytes before overhead, already beyond 512 MiB. This is the price of that overcomplete dense representation, not a lower bound on every sparse degree-one algorithm. At Delta even listing the coefficient basis is prohibitive. The degree cap makes the question finite but supplies no affordable pilot.

The relative chart is smaller: 48 outputs of degree at most four in 33 variables over a seventeen-parameter rational function field; padding is an affine map from 22 variables. At cutoff d its corresponding sizes are binom(48+d,48), binom(33+4d,33), binom(22+d,22). Symbolic rational-function coefficient growth in the base field must be included, not priced as constant-cost integers. The affine graph has 81 variables over that field. No manageable sparse h, global rank ceiling for a finite evaluation scheme, or closure-aware elimination basis has been identified.

**Decision:** zero pilots. A Jacobian rank of the determinant map would only sharpen image dimension and would not decide whether the 22-plane/actual padding lies in the closed image. Direct solvability checks would lose projection boundary points. Neither is a justified substitute for the missing certificate. No dense elimination, random hunt, or rank-only pilot is requested or scheduled.

Any later numerical check would need a separately concrete sparse candidate/identity, a new preregistration hash and the existing atomic exclusive lease, one process/thread, <=60 seconds and <=512 MiB, with all controls/failures counted toward the unchanged three-pilot/180-second cap. Those requirements are not satisfied merely by this theoretical finite cap. This session ends with the specified comparison OPEN.
