## 1. The three spaces

Let V=C^5, W=Sym^4(V*), phi(B)=det(sum x_i B_i), and D=closure(im phi). Let P=closure{l per_3(A(x))}, where l and the nine entries of A are arbitrary linear forms in five variables. Write R=closure{l C : C in Sym^3(V*)}; P is contained in R without any dominance premise.

Fix m independent nonzero vectors v_1,...,v_m, m=3,4,5, and extend them to a basis. A second transverse jet at v_i has functional span L_i=v_i^2 Sym^2 V. Indeed its fifteen coefficient functionals are nonzero scalar multiples of v_i^4, v_i^3 u_j, v_i^2 u_j u_k. Multiplication by v_i^2 is injective in Sym V. A change of complementary basis changes the fifteen coordinates invertibly and leaves L_i unchanged. Scaling v_i also leaves this space unchanged.

In the extended basis, put

    E_m={alpha in N^5 : |alpha|=4 and alpha_i>=2 for at least one i<=m},
    J_m=C^{E_m},  pi_m(F)=(c_alpha(F))_{alpha in E_m},
    K_m=ker pi_m=span{x^alpha : |alpha|=4, alpha_i<=1 for all i<=m}.

**PROVED.** Each L_i has 15 monomials; L_i intersect L_j is the line v_i^2 v_j^2. No monomial lies in three L_i. Since these are coordinate subspaces in this one basis, inclusion-exclusion is valid: dim J_m=15m-binom(m,2). Pairwise intersection formulas alone would not justify inclusion-exclusion for arbitrary subspaces; the simultaneous monomial basis does.

## 2. An actual-image determinant upper bound

**PROVED.** A dense subset of D is parametrized by the following polynomial fifty-parameter map:

    N(a,lambda,C,E,G)=a det(x1 I4+x2 diag(lambda1,...,lambda4)+x3 C+x4 E+x5 G),
    C_12=C_13=C_14=1.

There are 1+4+13+16+16=50 independent parameters. Here density, not just a count, is essential. On the nonempty dense open set of pencil space where B1 is invertible, put a=det B1 and A_j=B1^{-1}B_j. On the further dense open set where A2 has four distinct eigenvalues, diagonalize it by conjugation. Generically the first-row off-diagonal entries of A3 in this eigenbasis are nonzero. Conjugating by a diagonal matrix then sets these three entries to one; its scalar ambiguity is harmless. These conditions occur, for example, when A2 is diagonal with distinct entries and A3 has those three entries nonzero. Equivalently the failure sets are proper closed sets after the finite eigenvalue-ordering cover. Conjugation does not change a determinant.

Hence every image point from that dense open source belongs to im N. Conversely every N is an actual determinant: multiply its matrix on the left by diag(a,1,1,1), including a=0. If a polynomial vanishes on im N, its pullback under phi vanishes on a dense open subset of the irreducible affine source, hence everywhere. Therefore closure(im N)=D. This proves an upper bound on the actual image:

    dim closure(pi_m(D)) <= min(50,dim J_m).

No equality with 50, finite generic fiber, or generic Jacobian rank is inferred from the parameter count.

## 4. Why five is the decisive case

The omitted space has decreased to five explicitly known squarefree coefficients. For generic l, none of those directions preserves divisibility by l; hence a factor-preserving padding completion cannot use them at all. At the same time K_5 itself is a linear space of actual determinants, which creates a genuine projection-center/boundary issue. These two facts, proved in the supporting files, are new constraints beyond the earlier one- and two-center freedom. They motivate the single frozen five-center comparison; no frame hunt or alternative-matrix search was run.
