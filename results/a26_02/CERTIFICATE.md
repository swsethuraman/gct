## Two short hand lemmas used in the certificate

**Curve through the boundary point.** Let `G` be the irreducible projective graph closure, `U` a dense open, and `p∈G` a closed point. In an irreducible affine neighborhood of `p`, choose a nonzero regular `f` whose zero set contains the complement of `U` there. If dimension exceeds one, choose a general hyperplane through `p` that contains no irreducible component of the divisor `f=0`. Every dimension-one-lower component of the section through `p` is then outside `f=0`; choose one and repeat. Krull's principal ideal theorem gives the dimension drop. The resulting integral curve contains `p` and meets `U`. Normalizing it and localizing at a point over `p` gives the required DVR with residue field `C`. This proves the curve-selection step without assuming a finite limiting determinant matrix.

**A line on every smooth cubic threefold.** Pick `p∈C(C)`. Directions of lines through `p` form `P^3`. Expanding the cubic along a direction `v` gives `C(p+tv)=tL(v)+t²Q(v)+t³H(v)`. Smoothness makes the linear form `L` nonzero, so tangent directions form `P^2`. A conic `Q=0` and a cubic `H=0` in this `P^2` have a common complex point (including the case one restriction vanishes identically). The corresponding line lies in `C`. Thus the line used by the Clemens–Griffiths unirationality construction exists for every smooth `C`.

## Precision to B26-01's incidence argument

B26-01 S2 says a finite image is impossible because the corresponding kernels have dimension at most one. Rank-at-most-two matrix points have larger kernels, so that sentence alone is incomplete. The P4 argument in row 1 repairs it: a rational map from irreducible `P^3` into the finite rank-at-most-two locus has constant image `x`; then `M(x)u=0` on a dense open of `P^3`, whence `M(x)=0`. A generic five-tuple `B_i` is linearly independent, so this is impossible for projective `x`. Thus the incidence image meets rank three; unique kernels give generic injectivity, and characteristic zero gives birationality. This is a presentation repair within the same theorem, not a narrower result.

The original proof's claim that the total space `X` is normal is also sound: horizontal R1 follows from normality of the generic quartic, vertical R1 from row 2, and hypersurfaces in regular schemes are S2. Applying Theorem 5.3 to `X^ν` as above needs less and avoids relying on generic normality for the conclusion.
