## LMR, reconstruction, and the two meanings of rank

For the determinant at a rank-`n-1` matrix, reduce by left/right changes to `diag(0,I_(n-1))`. With tangent block `X=[[a,r],[c,E]]`, the quadratic term is

`a tr(E) - r c`.

Its Hessian has rank `2+2(n-1)=2n`. Congruence preserves rank, and closure of the rank condition extends `rank Hess(det_n)<=2n` to **all singular matrices**. For det4 this is eight. At rank `n-2` the quadratic term is the determinant of the two-by-two missing block, of rank four; at ranks at most `n-3` the Hessian is zero. These are pointwise matrix ranks, not dimensions of an ideal of equations on forms.

For any linear pencil, its polynomial Hessian is a linear pullback of this bilinear form. At a simple root of its characteristic polynomial, every 9-by-9 Hessian minor therefore vanishes. Monic division and density turn this into global polynomial equations in pencil coefficients, including repeated-root and dependent-direction cases. This is the LMR mechanism. Slot05's contribution is an exact reconstruction of its four directions in the packet's source conventions, with independence and finite-lift certificates.

LMR's Theorem 2.3.1 constructs remainder modules; for quartics, the flag module with `k=6` has degree 24 and weight `(65,17,2^7)`, while `k=7` gives degree 27 and weight `(73,19,2^8)`. Their discussion also allows planes not contained in the Hessian subspace, so one flag module is not an exhaustive list of remainder modules. Their local component theorem does not identify a global ideal with the determinant-closure ideal. [Landsberg–Manivel–Ressayre, §§2.2–2.3 and Theorem 3.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.4802).

By contrast, evaluation rank `r_X` in an `a`-dimensional highest-weight space gives `m_X>=r_X` and **`i_X<=a-r_X`**. A rank deficit is never a global ideal lower bound. For `D=m_pad-m_det=i_det-i_pad`, positivity requires a global determinant floor `q` and padding rank floor `r_pad` with `q+r_pad>a` in the **same finite cell**.

## Finite filtration and what is actually new modulo LMR

Let `J24` denote the homogeneous GL-stable ideal generated by the full known degree-24 9-minor LMR remainder equations. Extra weights or copies in its multiplication image remain equations from **the same ideal**. A proper novelty test at `(d,lambda)` is a nonzero class in `I_det(d,lambda)/J24(d,lambda)`, with multiplication-image kernels and overlaps included. Merely finding another tensor channel, another minor size, or another independent equation does not establish this.

Squared divisibility is a higher-corank consequence of the same Hessian geometry. On the squarefree chart, its coefficients lie in the discriminant saturation of the full ordinary-remainder ideal: `D=pQ+R`, `D'=tr(adj H H')`, and invertibility of p' modulo p imply `Q mod p=0` after localizing at `disc(p)`. This is **not** a proof of unsaturated ideal membership, and is not by itself an identification with `J24`.

A finite degree argument does distinguish one new class. For an unnormalized quartic G with leading coefficient c, normalize and depress by `t -> t-a1(x)/(4c)`. Write `q=G(t,e)/c` before depression. In the monic polynomial `q^2`, the coefficient dropping t-degree by i has denominator dividing `c^i` (indeed at most `c^2` for i>=2). Inductively, the coefficient of t^j in `t^k mod q^2` has denominator dividing `c^(k-j)`. Since `det Hess G` has coefficient degree 10 and t-degree at most 20, normalization and division give denominator at most `c^(30-j)`. The depression shear has determinant one; substituting its shift in a remainder of degree at most seven preserves this bound, since a term from degree l to j adds at most `c^-(l-j)`.

Therefore **`c^(30-j) S_j` is a polynomial of coefficient degree `30-j`**. Its highest-weight property follows from depression removing the first-row translations and from the two antisymmetrizations. In particular:

## Other primary mechanisms: derived limits, not a search list

**Higher curvature has two distinct meanings here.** The `p^(r-8)` divisibility statement concerns higher vanishing multiplicity of a determinant of the **ordinary second-derivative Hessian**. It uses no third- or fourth-derivative tensor. Genuine higher-derivative compatibility instead constrains those tensors jointly and can impose more than a Hessian-rank condition; it needs a separate derivation and a closure-valid polynomial certificate.

[Yabe, *Bi-polynomial rank and determinantal complexity*, arXiv:1504.00151v1, Definition 1.4 and Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.00151) is a primary higher-order route. For a degree-2k form, bi-polynomial rank is the minimum number of products of degree-k forms summing to it. For a zero x0 of p in D variables, the theorem gives `dc(p) >= b-rank(p_x0^(2k))/2^(2k-2) - 2(k-1)D^(k-1)`. This is ordinary affine determinantal complexity. Its rank-minimization formulation and real positive-semidefinite certificates do not automatically furnish complex polynomial equations on an orbit closure. One must control the closure of the low-bi-rank parameter image and eliminate its auxiliary factors; ordinary rank versus border rank cannot be conflated.

For the present homogeneous quartic, the fourth Taylor component is the same quartic at every basepoint. There is an immediate negative control: expanding per3 along its first row writes `z per3` as **three products of quadratics**, so its quartic bi-polynomial rank is at most three. For D=10, k=2, Yabe's displayed bound is therefore numerically unhelpful here (`b/4-20`); even the universal quadratic-monomial upper bound 55 would be insufficient to exceed dc=4. This does not refute the general higher-order method. It specifically rejects using the isolated fourth-order bi-rank as the next det4/padded-per3 test.

**Small mixed third/fourth-order next test.** At `M0=diag(0,I3)`, use the same linear blocks a,r,c,E in every Taylor component. Direct block expansion gives

```
F1 = a
F2 = a tr(E) - r c
F3 = a ((tr E)^2-tr(E^2))/2 - r ((tr E)I-E)c
F4 = a det(E) - r adj(E)c.
```

The shared E,r,c across F2,F3,F4 is the compatibility condition missing from separate derivative-rank tests. A bounded next control should use three chosen directions, expand the 24 determinant terms exactly, and verify all 34 nonconstant Taylor coefficients against these four formulas, then alter one cubic coefficient to reject the control. Cost estimate: under one second and 32 MiB, no geometry search. A three-direction success establishes the instrument only: its elimination ideal might even be zero, and no ten-variable equation follows from those controls.

Before promoting this route to an additional target, require a **nonzero polynomial elimination output** in universal Taylor coefficients, with an exact ideal-membership/substitution witness eliminating the shared a,r,c,E. To obtain an equation on quartic forms, also eliminate or polynomially clear the selected root/basepoint and normalization chart. The final required certificate is a finite-degree polynomial E in quartic coefficients with an exact identity `E(coeff(det(sum A_i x_i)))=0` for arbitrary matrix entries A_i, or an equivalent complete symbolic remainder witness followed by density; prove ambient nonzeroness, name its highest weight, and reduce it modulo the full specified LMR multiplication image. Separate padding evaluation/rank and finite filtration are still required. No general elimination was run: preflight the coefficient/support count first and stop if it exceeds the existing 60-second/512-MiB budget. Thus this is a well-defined compatibility control, not an asserted new equation family.

Higher multiplicity of a polynomial as a Hessian factor is also studied in [Cunha–Ramos–Simis, *Degenerations of the generic square matrix*, introduction and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.07681). That paper studies specified matrix degenerations; it does not license identities from a diagonal/symmetric specialization on every determinant pencil. Here the multiplicity-two statement was derived directly from corank and density.

The adjugate identity `adj(adj M)=det(M)^(n-2) M` is universal in matrix entries, but still contains the matrix realization. Turning it into equations on quartic coefficients requires elimination and checking the result modulo J24. No such coefficient equation or named additional module was certified here. Thus a universal claim `grad F=0 => rank Hess F<=4` for arbitrary det4 pencils is unjustified. The bound six follows, while rank-two matrix points give four.
