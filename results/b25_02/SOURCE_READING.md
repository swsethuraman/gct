## 1. Beauville (PRIMARY, arXiv v2) — the gate for this slot

Passages bound, with the Paper 2 claim each bears on:

- **(0.1)** "We have restricted our attention to smooth hypersurfaces"; "only curves and cubic
  surfaces admit generically a determinantal equation."
- **Cor. 1.8 + (1.9)** — "if Pic(X) is generated by O_X(1) the hypersurface can not be defined
  by a … determinant with [size] > 1 … we infer from the Noether-Lefschetz theorem that the
  generic hypersurface of degree d in P^n can be expressed in a non-trivial way as a determinant
  only if n = 2 or n = 3 and d ≤ 3." **Quinary cubics:** at n = 4, d = 3 a generic cubic
  threefold is not a non-trivial determinant. This supports Prop. 6.1's "does not extend to
  r = 5" sentence, now cited as `\cite[(1.9)]{Beau}`.
- **Cor. 6.4** — "A smooth cubic surface can be defined by an equation det M = 0, where M is a
  3 × 3-linear matrix. There are 72 such representations", credited to Grassmann [G] (1855).
  Supports Prop. 6.1's containment step, now `\cite[Cor.~6.4]{Beau}` (Grassmann UNREAD).
- **Cor. 6.6** (smooth determinantal quartic surfaces ⇔ contain a non-hyperelliptic genus-3
  sextic) — consistent with LLV's F1; not used in the paper.
- **Dimensions.** Beauville computes no dimension of the variety of linear determinantal
  hypersurfaces; the only dimension counts are for pfaffians (§7.6, §8.9, §9). **Prop. 2.1 is
  therefore not a re-proof of Beauville.** For r = 4 the dimension is published in LLV Cor. 3.1
  (now cited).
- **Nodes.** Beauville treats smooth hypersurfaces only; the singular locus of the generic
  *determinantal* member (Thm 7.1 Step 2, ν(n) nodes) is not in the paper. (8.3) discusses the
  singular locus of the universal *pfaffian* hypersurface (rank ≤ 2d−4, codimension 6), a
  different object. **Step 2 is not Beauville's.**
- **Quinary cubics / Thm 7.3.** Beauville's cubic threefolds are *smooth* and are represented as
  linear pfaffians (Prop. 8.5, Adler). Six-nodal determinantal cubic threefolds do not occur.
  **Thm 7.3 is not in Beauville.** A wider classical search (nodal cubic threefolds, Segre cubic
  literature) was **not** made; the paper now says so and claims no priority.
- Historical pointer only: Beauville's [D] Dickson 1921 "determined the general homogeneous forms
  which can be expressed as linear determinants" — UNREAD; not cited.

## 2. Kadish–Landsberg (PRIMARY, v1) — B1

**Thm 1.3:** "I_d(F_{n−m}(S^n W)) contains the isotypic component of S_π W in S^d(S^n W) for all
π = (p1, …, pd) with p1 < d(n − m). It does not contain a copy of any S_π W where
p1 ≥ min{d(n−1), dn−m}." Proof (§2): all monomials m_I vanish on (e1)^{n−m}h unless
p1 ≥ d(n−m).

With n − m = 1, F_1(S^n W) is exactly the reducible locus {ℓ·h}. The first assertion says the
**whole** λ-isotypic component lies in the ideal when λ1 < δ: coordinate-ring multiplicity **0**.
Paper 2's Cor. 5.3 printed `mult_λ C[R_r] = a(λ,δ)`, the opposite. **B1 confirmed at source**
(B24-10 §5.2 had accepted it on READ only). Also corrected: the corollary's "omits *some*
variable" must be "omits *every* variable" for Thm 5.1's criterion to apply (λ_i ≤ λ1 < δ).
